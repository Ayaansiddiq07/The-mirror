# The Mirror — judge-proof pitch review

## Read on the concept

The premise becomes clear when Gold reveals the same camera showing the same person, now across the full screen. The monochrome premium treatment supports the serious-to-satirical turn, and Silver/Gold controls give the team a concrete demo. End on the concise thesis: “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one.”

## Likely judge objections, ranked by risk

- **“Is this actually built, and what can you show live?” High risk if the camera fails or the demo feels like a mockup.** Lead with Silver-to-Gold so the screen change is visible. Camera permission is optional; enable the preview only if ready. If access is denied or unavailable, keep the local illustration visible and continue without apologizing. Rehearse both paths and the controls.
- **“Is this harmless, or are you asking for access or money?” High trust risk, easy to resolve.** Say plainly after the preview: “Camera permission is optional. The preview stays on this device, temporarily; it is never recorded or uploaded.” At pricing, state that every price and download claim is fictional and no purchase or download happens. Keep the free exit explicit. Avoid broader privacy claims unless they match the implementation.
- **“Why spend 30 seconds on the opening film?” Medium risk because it uses one sixth of the pitch before the app appears, but the assignment fixes the full 00:00–00:30 silent slot. Keep that slot. Make the visual progression brisk, reveal THE MIRROR at :25, and let the last five seconds land cleanly. Rehearse the cue into the product reveal.
- **“Are the prices, 25 GB download, and deletion fee real?” Medium risk if the gags resemble a paywall. Keep the 25 GB and 10 GB claims visibly marked fictional; label the ₹5,000 extras and all upgrade actions mock-only as clearly as Gold. Say once that no transaction or download occurs. Make the ₹2,000 deletion fee dismissible, with “CLOSE FOR FREE” as the clear action; do not stage a fake purchase confirmation.
- **“What is the actual point?” Medium risk if the close repeats setup details instead of landing the joke. State the thesis once: premium packaging can monetize a capability the phone already has. Leave a beat after the final line.

## Recommended three-minute arc

| Time | Speaker | Job and cue |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Keep the required full 30-second film: water, mirror timeline, layered phone reveal; title at :25. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Serious product reveal, then the turn: “The glass is conveniently the screen you already own.” Avoid unsupported “ultra-clear” claims. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Show Silver partial-screen, then Gold full-screen. Let the visual prove the joke. Add one privacy sentence; pause briefly for the permission prompt or toggle. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Silver free; Gold ₹2,000; ₹5,000 extras with the best one or two gags. Point to the fictional download/update copy, clarify no purchase/download, and make free exit legible. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | State the thesis, briefly confirm temporary local camera behavior and free exit, then close: “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That’s the whole point.” |

## Short answer for judges

**“We built a front-camera mirror demo: Silver shows a partial-screen reflection, and Gold expands it to full-screen. The optional preview is local and temporary; the upgrades, fees, and huge-download claims are fictional. It’s useless because it sells back a basic feature the phone already has.”**

## Rehearsal fixes

The spoken sections fit the allotted 2:30 only if the team preserves room for the demo and handoffs. Rehearse to exactly 3:00 with the actual deck, a single slide operator, and a no-camera run. If late, trim the extras list and repeated privacy/download lines; keep the full opening slot, demo, no-purchase/free-exit clarification, and final thesis. Let speaker handoffs happen on slide transitions. Mention the one-time first-run preview only if it appears in the demo; it adds little to the core joke.

## Assumptions

I treated the stated three-minute flow, full 30-second silent opening, and four-speaker sequence as fixed. The camera preview is optional, local, and temporary, and the pricing/download elements remain nonfunctional satire.
