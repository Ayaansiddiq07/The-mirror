# The Mirror: judge-proof pitch review

## The objections most likely to matter

| Objection | Risk | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| “Is this a real, working app or just a polished launch page?” | High. The script promises a live demo, so a failed camera permission prompt or an unexplained mock can undercut the reveal. | Rehearse the exact presentation device and browser. Start with Silver already visible; request camera access only on the explicit tap. If access fails, say “Camera access is optional; this illustration is our fallback” and toggle Silver/Gold anyway. Avoid calling the preview “4K” or “instant” as a measured technical claim. |
| “Are these prices or downloads real?” | High if the audience has to infer it. The ₹2,000 deletion joke and fictional 25 GB/10 GB claims could sound coercive or like a real download. | Put “FICTIONAL · NO PURCHASE OR DOWNLOAD” beside the claims, label both upgrade controls “MOCK ONLY,” and say the clarification as the gag appears. State that closing is always free. |
| “Why should we trust the camera?” | High enough to address plainly; privacy is part of the product’s behavior, not a punchline. | Say it once during the demo: “The preview stays on this device temporarily; we do not record, upload, or retain it.” Do not over-explain implementation. Keep the camera-denied fallback visible. |
| “Does the joke arrive too late?” | Moderate. A 30-second cinematic opener uses one-sixth of the pitch. The serious-to-satirical reveal is the premise, so it earns its time only if the first laugh/recognition comes promptly afterward. | Keep the silent opening, but make it a brisk visual progression and land THE MIRROR title at 00:25. At 00:30, make “the glass is the screen you already own” the turn. Do not add narration or more history. |
| “Can four speakers fit the clock?” | Moderate. The current demo and closing need pauses and interface actions; their scripts leave little room for fumbling. | Rehearse to the listed caps with a visible timer. Assign one speaker per post-film beat, with the next speaker ready at each slide change. Trim feature lists before cutting the privacy line, mock-only clarification, or final point. |

A useful comparison is a product-launch keynote: the trailer can establish the premium mood, but the live demo must prove the product and the final line must explain why the launch was absurd. The analogy breaks at the stakes: this app must make its fictional fees and its real camera behavior unmistakable. Treat those as audience-facing product facts, not fine print.

## Recommended three-minute arc

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film.** Water → polished metal → glass → phone. Title at 00:25; silent throughout.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal.** Sell the premium tone for one sentence, then reveal the “glass” is the existing screen.
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo.** Show Silver, switch to Gold, land the same-camera/same-person joke. Keep the fallback ready and privacy sentence short.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing.** Silver ₹0, Gold ₹2,000 once, extras ₹5,000; make every control and outrageous spec visibly fictional. Pause briefly for the “unlimited angles (one)” joke.
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close.** Confirm no data retention and free exit, then end on the reason it is useless. Leave a beat before “Thank you.”

## Tighter spoken version

**00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1**  
“Introducing The Mirror: a premium reflection experience, powered by the front camera already in your phone. Instant, ultra-clear, thoughtfully designed. We even engineered the glass—which is, conveniently, the screen you already own.”

**01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2**  
“Silver gives your reflection a tasteful half-screen window, leaving the other half for nothing. Gold unlocks the whole screen. Same camera, same person, same phone; we have simply given the empty space ambition. Camera access is optional. The preview stays on this device temporarily: no recording, upload, or retention. If access is denied, this illustration is our fallback.”

**01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3**  
“Silver is free: half a screen, all the reflection. Gold is ₹2,000 once to unlock the rest of your own screen. The ₹5,000 extras bundle adds a confidence filter, unlimited angles—which is one angle—and a cloth for the glass that was already there. These are mock prices; no purchase happens. Our fictional spec sheet claims a 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months. No files download. The forecast is imaginary; the margins are excellent.”

**02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4**  
“We built The Mirror to show how a basic feature can be dressed up as a premium product. There is even a fake ₹2,000 deletion fee; dismiss it and closing stays free. No personal data is kept. We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the whole point of useless software. Thank you.”

The revised spoken copy is about 180 words after the film, leaving room for deliberate pacing and the live toggle. Keep the ₹2,000 Gold and ₹5,000 extras controls visibly marked “MOCK ONLY”; the original page explicitly marks Gold but its extras button says only “ADD PREMIUM EXTRAS.” The spoken copy and on-screen disclaimer should agree at the moment each gag appears.

## Short answer for judges

“We built a phone app that uses the front camera as a local, temporary mirror, then satirizes premium software by charging in mock UI for half-screen versus full-screen reflection and imaginary extras. It is useless because the phone already has a front camera and screen; the joke is the productization around something it can already do.”

## Assumptions

I treated the pitch script and the slide notes in `index.html` as the current intended presentation, and the product/demo details in the brief as requirements. I have not verified the app on the presentation device or tested its camera and fallback behavior; those claims should be rehearsed and checked before the pitch.
