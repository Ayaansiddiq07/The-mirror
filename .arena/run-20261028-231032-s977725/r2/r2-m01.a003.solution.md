# The Mirror — pitch review

## Outline

1. Protect the serious premium reveal and the simple Silver-to-Gold visual joke.
2. Assess judge-facing risks against the supplied script and implementation: what was built, camera privacy, fictional pricing, trial behavior, and three-minute pacing.
3. Give practical wording and a timed four-speaker arc, including a concise answer to what the team built and why it is useless.

## What the team should protect

Keep the restrained monochrome reveal and the serious-to-satirical turn. The clearest joke is that Silver shows a partial-screen reflection and Gold sells the remaining screen space while using the same camera. A judge should understand it by watching the switch, without a long explanation.

## Likely judge questions and risk

- **“What did you actually build, and can I try it?” — High risk if the presentation site is described as a finished phone app.** The materials identify a presentation site with an interactive camera demo, Silver/Gold controls, mock pricing, and a free-exit joke. Call it a browser demo/prototype unless a separate phone app is available. Rehearse the camera path and the camera-denied illustration fallback.
- **“Does the camera stop when I leave, and is anything saved?” — High trust risk unless the interaction and explanation agree.** The preview is local and temporary, and the code stops its tracks on slide changes, when the page is hidden, and on unload. But the “CLOSE THE APP — FREE, ALWAYS” button first opens the deletion-fee dialog; that click alone does not stop the preview. The dialog’s “CLOSE FOR FREE” action does. Recommend stopping the camera as soon as the exit dialog opens. Until that is changed, say precisely: “Permission is optional; the preview is local and not recorded or uploaded. Opening the exit joke does not close the preview; choose ‘CLOSE FOR FREE’ to end it.” Keep the illustration fallback ready.
- **“Are the prices and giant download real?” — Moderate risk if the joke is not unmistakable.** Silver is free, Gold is a mock one-time ₹2,000 upgrade, and extras are mock UI at ₹5,000. The 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months are fictional and must trigger no download or purchase. Label the extras control itself as mock, and make its confirmation say the extras were “pretended” or “imaginary”; a toast saying they were “activated” can briefly read as a real action. Speaker 3 should say once that all charges and downloads are fictional.
- **“Is there really one trial?” — Moderate risk if presented as implemented.** The task defines this as a one-time first-run preview, but the reviewed script does not establish that behavior. Show it only if the build implements it; otherwise omit the trial claim.
- **“Does the film earn 30 seconds, and can four speakers fit?” — Moderate risk.** The 30-second silent opening is a required part of the assigned flow and sets up the serious reveal. Preserve it, rehearse the title reveal and handoffs, and do not spend the close repeating pricing, download, and privacy disclaimers already covered. The existing spoken sections are brisk; time them aloud with the live toggles and pauses, then trim feature-list wording if they overrun.

The ₹2,000 deletion fee is a dismissible joke, and closing must always be free. Keep that interaction and make the free route obvious. For the privacy behavior, the strongest practical fix is to stop the camera when the exit dialog opens, so the button’s promise and the preview lifecycle match.

## Focused three-minute pitch

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film:** water reflection → mirror history → layered phone → title. No narration; reveal the title at 00:25 and hand off cleanly.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal:** deliver the serious premium introduction, then land on: “The glass is conveniently the screen you already own.”
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo:** show Silver, switch to Gold, and say: “Same camera, same person, same phone; Gold just sells the empty space.” Add one accurate, concise local-preview/privacy line and explain when the preview ends. If permission is denied, use the illustration and continue.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing:** explain free Silver, mock one-time ₹2,000 Gold, and mock ₹5,000 extras. Point to the fictional-download disclaimer and say there is no purchase or download. Do not claim a trial unless it is demonstrably implemented.
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close:** state what the team built, why it is useless, and thank the judges. Avoid repeating prior disclaimers.

**Short answer to “what did you build and why is it useless?”**

“We built a browser demo of a front-camera mirror with Silver and Gold modes, mock premium prices, and a free exit. It is useless because your phone already has the camera and screen to do the same thing; the app sells the empty space as an upgrade.”

**Suggested closing line:** “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the point of useless software. Thank you.”

**Assumptions:** The presentation site is the artifact shown to judges; no separate phone app or implemented one-time first-run preview has been established in the reviewed materials. The camera behavior described above is based on the reviewed app code and interface. No unstated competition rubric is assumed.
