# The Mirror: pitch review

## What judges are most likely to challenge

1. **Is there a working product behind the launch film?** This is the highest presentation risk: a polished reveal can read as a concept-only pitch. Make the Silver-to-Gold change the first concrete proof. Have the app open before the demo, switch modes immediately, and keep the local fallback ready so a denied permission or slow camera never stalls the room. Say plainly that Gold changes the screen area, not the camera or image.
2. **Is the camera genuinely private?** “Nothing is recorded or saved” is reassuring, but repeated privacy claims consume time and may invite follow-up. State once, during the demo: “The preview stays on this device for this session; we don’t record, upload, or retain it. Camera permission is optional; the illustration is the fallback.” This is a concrete answer, not a broad privacy promise.
3. **Are the fees and huge download real?** ₹2,000, ₹5,000, a ₹2,000 deletion fee, 25 GB, and 10 GB updates can look like deceptive UX if the joke label is missed. Keep “SATIRICAL MOCK UI · NO PURCHASE OR DOWNLOAD” beside every relevant control and the download claims. Make the extras CTA explicitly mock too. Show “CLOSE FOR FREE” at the same time as the dismissible deletion-fee gag; never delay or obstruct exit.
4. **Does the final point land, and can four people fit?** The close currently repeats privacy and fictional-download explanations after pricing, then arrives at its best line. Remove repeated caveats from speech once they are clearly labeled on screen. Save the last breath for “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one.” Rehearse with the film, two mode taps, and transitions included; the close is the easiest section to overrun.

## Options and choice

| Choice | Clarity | Demo confidence | Timing | Trade-off |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Keep the current script verbatim | Good | Good if demo works | Tight close | Preserves all gags, but repeats assurances and buries the punchline. |
| Keep the 30-second film; trim repeated explanation and make mock labels explicit | Strong | Strong | Best | Gives up a little feature-list banter for a cleaner ending and safer joke framing. |
| Shorten the film to make more room for speech | Strong | Strong | Easiest | More spoken room, but violates the specified 30-second opening slot and weakens the serious reveal. |

**Recommendation:** choose the second option. The fixed film and monochrome premium reveal are part of the brief. Spend the saved words on live proof and a clean closing beat, not additional disclaimers.

## Recommended three-minute arc

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film.** Keep the water-to-phone progression restrained and legible; title at about 00:25. Silence supports the serious reveal and avoids audio-rule risk.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal.** Deliver the premium language straight, then land the screen-is-the-glass line as the first wink.
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo.** Open with Silver, tap Gold, pause for the visual difference, and state that it is the same reflection. Give the local-only privacy sentence once. Keep camera permission optional and continue immediately on the illustration if denied.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing.** Make the ₹2,000 Gold upgrade and ₹5,000 extras readable as jokes. Put the fictional 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months in a clearly labeled mock card; say they trigger no download. No payment flow.
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close.** Briefly connect premium packaging to the point of useless software, mention that deletion is a dismissible joke and exit is free, then finish on the existing mirror line. Avoid adding technical detail here.

**Short answer: what did you build, and why is it useless?** “We built a temporary, on-device front-camera mirror with Silver and full-screen Gold. It adds nothing a phone’s camera cannot already do; the joke is charging for the screen you already own.”

## Assumptions

I treated the 30-second silent film and five timing blocks as fixed requirements, and the stated local-only camera behavior and working fallback as implemented. The 25 GB and update claims should be visibly labeled fictional wherever they appear. No unstated judging rubric is assumed.
