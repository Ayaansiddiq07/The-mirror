# The Mirror: judge-proof pitch review

## Read on the concept

The core joke is clear and demoable: Silver and Gold change how much of the screen shows the same ordinary front-camera reflection, while the product language and prices inflate that trivial change. The monochrome premium reveal supports the satire. Keep the existing final idea—“We made a mirror for a phone that already had one”—as the punchline.

## Likely judge objections and practical fixes

- **“Is this a real app or just a mockup?” — Genuinely risky.** Use the live camera and tier controls to show the product working: toggle Silver to Gold on cue. Keep a local illustration ready if camera permission is denied or the camera is unavailable. Say once, plainly: “The preview is temporary and stays on this device; we don’t record, upload, or retain it.” Camera access is optional.
- **“Are those charges or downloads real?” — Genuinely risky if unclear.** The ₹2,000 Gold upgrade, ₹5,000 extras, 25 GB download, 10 GB updates every two months, and ₹2,000 deletion fee are fictional satire. Keep “MOCK ONLY” and “FICTIONAL — NO DOWNLOADS OR PURCHASES” readable on the relevant UI. Say “No purchase happens.” The deletion joke must be dismissible and “CLOSE FOR FREE” must remain available; do not delay or block exit.
- **“Does the joke need explaining?” — Some risk.** State the premise in the reveal, establish the privacy point during the demo, and clarify the fictional pricing during its beat. Avoid repeating every safety detail in the close; let it land on why the app is useless.
- **“Does the 30-second silent film earn its time?” — A real pacing risk, not a reason to change the slot.** It uses one-sixth of the pitch before judges see the app. Preserve the required 00:00–00:30 silent opening and make its visual progression purposeful: mirror history, phone reveal, then title. Pace the title reveal to support the handoff at 00:30. Rehearse the film as part of the three-minute whole.
- **“Can four speakers fit?” — Rehearsal risk.** Toggle delays, laughter, slide cues, and handoffs take time. Rehearse with a hard 03:00 timer, including the camera-denied fallback and transitions. Trim feature lists first; keep the privacy assurance, fictional/no-purchase clarification, and final point clear.

## Recommended three-minute arc

| Time | Speaker | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Premium visual build from reflection to phone and title; complete the full fixed slot. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Serious product reveal, then the first hint that the “glass” is the screen already owned. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Toggle Silver to Gold, show that the same reflection fills more screen, then give the one-sentence privacy assurance. Use the local illustration if camera access is denied. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Escalate through the fictional prices and extras. Keep the download/update claims marked fictional and say no transaction occurs. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | Give a concise statement of what the app demonstrates, optionally point out that the deletion joke is dismissible and exit is free, then end on the existing mirror-for-a-phone line. |

**Short answer to “what did you build and why is it useless?”** “We built a phone app that turns its front camera into a mirror, then pretends to sell screen space and imaginary extras as premium upgrades. It’s useless because the phone already has a screen and a camera—and the same reflection.”

## Assumptions

The prices, download size, update cadence, and deletion charge are fictional UI jokes only. They trigger no payment or download; camera preview is temporary and local, with no recording, upload, or retained personal data. Closing is always free. The 30-second silent opening, its following time boundaries, and the four-speaker sequence are fixed constraints. No unstated judging rubric is assumed.
