# The Mirror — pitch review

## Likely judge objections and fixes

- **“Is this a built app or just a camera preview with pricing jokes?” — Real risk.** State what works before the gag: “We built a local front-camera mirror with two display modes.” Then visibly switch Silver from partial-screen to Gold full-screen. That concrete change is the demo’s proof.
- **“What happens if the camera is denied?” — Real demo risk.** Keep the local illustration on screen and say, “Camera access is optional; this illustration shows the same Silver-to-Gold change.” Rehearse this path so a permission prompt cannot derail the demo.
- **“What happens to the camera feed?” — Real trust risk.** Say once, while demonstrating: “The preview is temporary and stays on this device. We do not record, upload, or retain camera data.” Confirm this exactly matches the implementation.
- **“Are the prices and download claims real?” — Real clarity risk.** Make the mock nature legible on screen and say the prices and file-size claims are fictional; no purchase or download occurs. Keep Gold’s ₹2,000 upgrade clearly one-time, the ₹2,000 deletion fee dismissible, and “Close for free” visible. The claimed 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months must remain mock UI only.
- **“Is the 30-second film worth the time?” — Pacing risk, not a reason to break the assigned format.** It uses one sixth of the pitch before naming the product, so make it visually legible and use it to set the serious premium tone. Keep it fully silent for 00:00–00:30; hold the title until 00:30 and start speech then.
- **“Can four speakers fit three minutes?” — Manageable with a timed rehearsal.** Keep the four handoffs at :30, 1:00, 1:45, and 2:25. Rehearse including the Silver-to-Gold switch and pauses. If over time, trim the extras list and repeated explanation before cutting the working-product statement, privacy assurance, or final point.

## Focused pitch structure

| Time | Speaker | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1 (silent) | Water reflection → mirror history → phone reveal. Title appears near :25 and stays through :30. No speech or audio. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Serious premium reveal; end on the screen already being there. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Identify the working local mirror; show Silver partial-screen then Gold full-screen; give the privacy line. If camera access fails, use the illustration fallback. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Silver free; Gold mock one-time ₹2,000; mock ₹5,000 extras; label pricing and file claims fictional. Show that no purchase/download occurs, deletion is dismissible, and exit is free. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | Give the concise reason the product is useless and thank the audience; add no new claims. |

## Suggested lines

**Reveal:** “Introducing The Mirror: an elegant front-camera reflection experience, powered by the camera already on your phone. We engineered every detail, including the glass—which is conveniently the screen you already own.”

**Demo:** “We built a local front-camera mirror with two display modes. Silver gives you part of the screen; Gold gives you all of it.” After the switch: “Same camera, same reflection, same phone—now the empty space has ambition.” Then: “The preview is temporary and stays on this device. We do not record, upload, or retain camera data.”

**Pricing:** “Silver is free. Gold is a fictional one-time ₹2,000 to unlock the rest of your own screen. The ₹5,000 extras and our 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months are fictional mock UI; nothing is purchased or downloaded. Even the fake deletion fee is dismissible. Closing stays free.”

**Close:** “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the whole point of useless software. Thank you.”

**Short answer to “what did you build and why is it useless?”** “We built a temporary, local front-camera mirror with partial-screen Silver and full-screen Gold modes. It is useless because Gold jokes about charging you to use the rest of the screen to show the same reflection.”

## Assumptions

The supplied timings are fixed, the one trial is a one-time first-run preview, and the team can verify that the privacy description matches the implementation. This review uses the supplied assignment and does not assume other judging criteria.
