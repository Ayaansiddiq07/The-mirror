# The Mirror — pitch review

## Recommendation

Keep the serious monochrome reveal and the 30-second silent film: it gives the joke a clean setup, and the visual is already framed as restrained rather than noisy. The user's likely path is simple—watch, see Silver/Gold, understand the fee joke, leave—so make each demo action and each fake charge unmistakable without adding explanatory detours.

## Likely judge questions and risk

- **“What is actually built, and can we try it?” — High risk if the presentation is mistaken for the app.** `index.html` is a polished pitch deck with a real browser camera preview, Silver/Gold controls, mock pricing, and a dismissible free-exit dialog. Say that plainly: the demo is a browser prototype; the camera feed is temporary and local. Have the camera-denied illustration ready and rehearse the demo without granting permission. The build prompt calls for an offline, local-assets demo, but `index.html` loads Google Fonts and `app.js` can request an Unsplash image. Remove those requests before claiming offline/self-contained operation, or describe the current deck accurately.
- **“Are the prices and huge download real?” — High risk unless the gag reads before anyone clicks.** The Gold button says “MOCK ONLY” and the screen has a global satire disclaimer, but Extras says “ADD PREMIUM EXTRAS”; its toast says extras were “activated.” Mark both buttons as mock UI and make the toast explicitly fictional. The 25 GB download and 10 GB update claim appear in the speaker notes/script, not in the pricing screen itself. Put them in visible pricing copy with “fictional” beside them, and say once that no download or purchase occurs. Keep the deletion fee dismissible and the free exit obvious, as the current dialog does.
- **“Does the camera collect anything?” — Moderate risk; answer with proof, briefly.** The implementation uses `getUserMedia` and a local `video.srcObject`, with no recording/upload code and stops tracks on slide change, hidden page, exit, or unload. Put “LOCAL PREVIEW · NO RECORDING OR UPLOAD” beside the camera control; mention the illustration fallback. This is stronger than a generic privacy promise and takes little pitch time.
- **“Is the joke clear, and does the film earn 30 seconds?” — Moderate risk.** The serious reveal is effective, but the joke should land by the Gold switch: it is the same camera and the same reflection, with the rest of the screen priced as an upgrade. Keep the opening at 30 seconds as assigned; rehearse the title reveal and advance cue so the film does not run into Speaker 1’s 30-second section.
- **“Will four speakers fit, and what is the point?” — Moderate risk.** The script has four speaking turns and matching slide timings. Speaker 4 currently repeats the no-purchase, fictional-size, deletion-fee, and privacy explanations after those gags have already appeared; that makes the final 35 seconds the likeliest rushed section. Put the 25 GB/update line with pricing, leave the deletion joke on screen, and let Speaker 4 give the point and close. The build prompt also specifies a one-time first-run preview; it is neither in this script nor evident in the current `app.js`. If asked, do not claim it is built—add it to the demo or remove it from the product promise.

## Suggested three-minute arc

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film:** reflection history, layered phone, title. Advance on the title.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal:** deliver the premium setup; end on “the glass is the screen you already own.”
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo:** show Silver, switch to Gold, land “same camera, same person, same phone.” Add the short local/no-recording line; use the illustration if camera permission fails.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing:** free Silver, ₹2,000 Gold, ₹5,000 extras; one punchline about 25 GB and 10 GB updates, explicitly labeled fictional. State once: no payment or download happens.
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close:** one brief privacy/permission line if not already shown, then “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the point of useless software. Thank you.” Protect a pause after the final sentence.

**Clear answer:** “We built a browser demo of a front-camera mirror with Silver and Gold modes, mock premium pricing, and a free exit. It is useless because the phone already has a camera and screen that do the job; the app sells empty screen space as an upgrade.”

**Assumptions:** The deck is the artifact shown to judges, and the actual phone-app build is not established by the supplied files. No specific judging rubric is assumed.
