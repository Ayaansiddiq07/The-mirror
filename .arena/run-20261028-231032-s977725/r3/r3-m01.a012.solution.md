# The Mirror: judge-proof pitch review

## Likely judge objections and risk

1. **Does the joke land, and can the team demo the app? High risk.** The serious reveal should turn clearly satirical by the first spoken section. In the demo, visibly switch Silver from partial-screen to Gold full-screen. Rehearse the transitions and have the camera-denied fallback ready; do not claim features the team cannot show.

2. **Is the camera explanation trustworthy? High risk if unclear.** Say once that the preview is temporary and local, with no recording, upload, or retained personal data. Permission is optional; show the local fallback if access is denied. Keep this concise so the explanation supports the demo without taking over the joke.

3. **Could mock fees or downloads be mistaken for real actions? High risk.** Mark all prices and fictional download/update claims as satire. State that there is no checkout and no download or update runs. Keep the ₹2,000 deletion fee dismissible and show that closing is free. Keep the joke in the UI: do not lock the device, collect personal data, use rapid flashing, or play loud audio. “One trial” means a one-time first-run preview.

4. **Does the 30-second film earn its time? Meaningful risk, not a flaw by itself.** It establishes the premium tone, but delays proof of the app. Keep the silent slot and bring the actual app into the film by 00:20, then hold THE MIRROR title through 00:30. If the film remains only atmosphere, shorten the atmospheric sequence within the slot. No narration or loud audio is needed.

5. **Can four speakers and transitions fit? Manageable.** Reserve three seconds for each of the three handoffs and allow the mode changes to read on screen. Use the word ceilings below as caps, not targets; rehearse against the clock. Cut optional feature jokes first, while retaining the privacy, no-purchase, and free-exit clarifications.

## Focused three-minute pitch

Timings include transitions and demo actions. The film remains silent. Keep the monochrome premium look through the reveal, then let the mock UI make the satire visible.

**00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1:** “Introducing The Mirror: a premium reflection experience, powered by the front camera already on your phone. We refined every detail, including the glass—which is conveniently the screen you already own.”

**01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2:** “Silver gives your reflection part of the screen. Now Gold fills the whole display. Same camera, same reflection; we simply charged the empty space with ambition. The preview is temporary and local, with no recording, upload, or retention. If camera access is denied, we can show the fallback.”

**On screen:** Toggle Silver, then Gold. Use the local fallback illustration if camera access is denied or unavailable. Keep the preview temporary and local.

**01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3:** “Silver is free. Gold is a mock one-time ₹2,000 upgrade to unlock your own screen. The mock ₹5,000 extras include unlimited angles—which is still one angle. These prices are jokes: there is no checkout. Our fictional 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months never download or update anything.”

**On screen:** Clearly label prices, the 25 GB download, and the “10 GB updates every two months” claim as fictional. No purchase, download, or update runs. If the dismissible ₹2,000 deletion-fee gag appears, dismiss it and visibly choose “Close for free.” Do not linger on the interface.

**02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4:** “We built a temporary, local front-camera mirror. It is useless because your phone already has a camera and screen; Gold sells you the rest of your screen. The download and deletion fee are jokes. Closing is free. That is the point of useless software. Thank you.”

## What we built and why it is useless

“We built a phone app that shows a temporary, local front-camera mirror, with partial-screen Silver and full-screen Gold. It is useless because your phone already has the camera and screen, and Gold charges to use more of the screen.”

## Assumptions

The five listed time blocks and silent 30-second film are fixed. “One trial” means a one-time first-run preview. This is a pitch review; it does not independently verify the implementation. Word ceilings leave room for the three handoffs and demo pauses; rehearse with a stopwatch and trim optional jokes if needed.
