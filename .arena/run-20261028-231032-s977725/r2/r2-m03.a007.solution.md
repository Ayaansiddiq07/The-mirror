# The Mirror pitch review

## Likely judge objections and risk

- **“Is there a real, demoable app?” — genuine risk if the team only describes the joke.** Show the implemented Silver partial-screen and Gold full-screen modes changing the same preview. Have the camera-denied illustration ready and continue without delay if permission is unavailable. Describe only behavior the app actually implements.
- **“What happens to camera data?” — genuine trust risk.** Say directly: “Camera permission is optional. The preview stays local and temporary; we do not record, upload, or retain it.” Use the illustration if permission is denied. Keep this explanation short and consistent with the implementation.
- **“Are these charges and download claims real?” — genuine clarity risk if the controls resemble a real checkout.** Label the Gold and extras prices “MOCK ONLY · NO PURCHASE.” Mark the 25 GB download, 10 GB updates every two months, and deletion fee as fictional mock UI. Make the deletion-fee joke dismissible and show “CLOSE FOR FREE.” If trial language appears, call it a “ONE-TIME FIRST-RUN PREVIEW”; otherwise leave it out.
- **“Does the opening film earn 30 seconds?” — a pacing tradeoff.** Keep the serious premium reveal, make the phone recognizable before the spoken line, and land THE MIRROR by 00:25. Use measured cuts and gentle transitions; no rapid flashing. If rehearsal runs long, trim the montage before cutting the live demo or privacy clarification.
- **“Can four speakers fit three minutes?” — genuine rehearsal risk.** The current close repeats privacy, download sizes, the fee gag, and the thesis after those claims have appeared in the demo/pricing beats. Keep the download and fee as labeled visual jokes during pricing, then let Speaker 4 deliver the point and thank the audience. Rehearse with mode changes and pauses included.

The strongest reveal is that the “glass” is the phone screen already owned; “charged the empty space with ambition” is the best demo punchline. Preserve the monochrome premium tone and let the absurdity come from the contrast. Keep claims like “AI-ENHANCED YOU” visibly in the fictional mock interface.

## Focused pitch structure

| Time | Speaker | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Premium reflection-to-phone film, title by 00:25. No rapid flashing; use gentle transitions and silent audio. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Reveal the screen was already there. One beat for the joke. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Toggle Silver, then Gold; deliver the “same reflection, more ambition” line. State the local, temporary camera behavior and fallback briefly. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Silver free, Gold ₹2,000 once, extras ₹5,000. Clearly say all prices are fictional and no purchase occurs. Keep download/update and dismissible deletion-fee gags labeled on screen, not in extra narration. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | State what was built and why it is useless; finish on the thesis. Avoid introducing new features or repeating the pricing jokes. |

Suggested compact close: “We built a phone app that turns its front camera into a mirror, then sells the same reflection back in premium tiers. The preview is temporary and local; the upgrades are jokes, and closing is free. The phone already had a mirror. That is the point.” Leave a pause before the final sentence.

## Short answer: what did you build, and why is it useless?

“We built a phone app that turns its front camera into a mirror, then sells the same reflection back in premium tiers. Silver uses part of the screen; Gold uses all of it. The preview is temporary and local, and the upgrades are jokes. It is useless because the phone already had a mirror.”

## Assumptions

The listed segment times are fixed, and the stated Silver/Gold, camera fallback, and mock-price behavior are intended to be implemented. The film can be edited with measured cuts and gentle transitions. The team can show fictional download, update, and deletion-fee claims as mock UI without narrating them. No unstated judging rubric is assumed.
