# The Mirror — pitch review

## What the team should protect

Keep the restrained monochrome reveal and the serious-to-satirical turn. The core gag is easy to grasp once Silver becomes Gold: the reflection stays the same while the empty screen space is sold as an upgrade. The user's path is simple—watch, try the mirror, see the mock prices, and leave—so keep the demo and explanation focused on those steps.

## Likely judge questions and risk

- **“What did you actually build, and can I try it?” — High risk if the browser deck is presented as a finished phone app.** The supplied implementation is a presentation site with an interactive camera demo, Silver/Gold controls, mock pricing, and a free-exit joke. Call it a browser prototype unless a separate phone app is available. Rehearse both the live camera path and the camera-denied illustration fallback.
- **“Does the camera save or send my image?” — Moderate risk; answer with the behavior, not just a promise.** The demo requests camera access and uses a local video preview; its code stops the tracks on slide changes, page hiding, exit, and unload. Say: “Permission is optional; the preview stays on this device and is not recorded or uploaded.” Point out the illustration fallback. Do not over-explain the implementation.
- **“Are these prices and the huge download real?” — Moderate risk if someone misses the joke.** The pricing slide already shows the ₹2,000 Gold and ₹5,000 extras prices as mock UI, and visibly labels the 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months as entirely fictional, with no downloads or purchases. Speaker 3 should point to that existing disclaimer and say once that nothing is charged or downloaded. The Gold button says “MOCK ONLY”; the extras button says “ADD PREMIUM EXTRAS” and its toast says “Premium extras activated,” so label the extras button as mock too and make the toast say the fictional extras were “pretended” or “imaginary.”
- **“Is the one trial real?” — Moderate risk if it is described as a product promise.** The assignment defines it as a one-time first-run preview, but that behavior is not evident in the supplied script or app.js. If the demo implements it, add one clear sentence and show it; otherwise do not claim it is present.
- **“Does the 30-second film earn its time, and can four speakers fit?” — Moderate risk.** The film is assigned a fixed 30 seconds and sets up the serious reveal, so retain it, but rehearse the title reveal and handoff exactly to protect the remaining beats. Speaker 4's current close repeats the fictional download, deletion-fee, and privacy explanations already covered; cut those repeats and use the final 35 seconds for the point and a clean ending.

The deletion-fee joke is already dismissible and explicitly says exit is free. Keep that interaction, and make sure the close makes the free exit obvious if it is shown.

## Focused three-minute pitch

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film:** water reflection → mirror history → layered phone → title. No narration; advance on the title.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal:** give the premium introduction and land on “the glass is the screen you already own.”
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo:** show Silver, switch to Gold, and say, “Same camera, same person, same phone.” Add one short local-preview/privacy line. Continue with the illustration if camera access is denied.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing:** explain free Silver, one-time ₹2,000 Gold, and ₹5,000 extras. Point at the existing fictional-download disclaimer; say no payment or download occurs. Keep the one-trial claim out unless the first-run preview is actually demoable.
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close:** briefly name what was built, why it is useless, and thank the judges. Do not repeat earlier disclaimers.

**Short answer to “what did you build and why is it useless?”**  
“We built a browser demo of a front-camera mirror with Silver and Gold modes, mock premium prices, and a free exit. It is useless because your phone already has the camera and screen to do the same thing; the app sells the empty space as an upgrade.”

**Suggested closing line:** “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the point of useless software. Thank you.”

**Assumptions:** The supplied presentation site is the artifact shown to judges; no separate phone app or first-run preview implementation has been established in the materials reviewed. No unstated judging rubric is assumed.
