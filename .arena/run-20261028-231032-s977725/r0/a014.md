# The Mirror — judge-proof pitch review

The concept has a clean satirical engine: the app does something a phone already does, then sells back screen space and imaginary extras. Keep the polished, serious reveal; make the first unmistakable joke land during the demo, and make every fictional charge visibly harmless.

## The objections most likely to matter

- **“Is there a real app here, or just a launch video?” — genuinely risky.** The script describes the gag well but leaves little time to establish what is functional. In the demo, visibly switch Silver to Gold and show the same reflection expanding. If permission is denied, use the built-in illustration and still demonstrate the mode change. Call this a working front-camera preview, not a product video. Don't add claims about engineering beyond what you can show.
- **“Are these fees or downloads real?” — genuinely risky if the UI is ambiguous.** The ₹2,000 Gold and ₹5,000 extras are mock prices; the 25 GB download and 10 GB updates are fictional. Say “mock” before touching the buttons, keep a clear on-screen “NO PURCHASES OR DOWNLOADS” label visible, and ensure the buttons only produce a joke confirmation. Present the ₹2,000 deletion fee as an optional, dismissible gag and point out “CLOSE FOR FREE.” Never make the fee appear to block exit.
- **“What happens to the camera feed?” — manageable, but trust depends on precision.** Say the preview is temporary and stays on-device; nothing is recorded, uploaded, or retained. Invite camera access only as an optional demo action, and keep the fallback ready. One concise, accurate statement is stronger than repeating privacy claims in the demo and close.
- **“Does the joke arrive too late?” — a real pacing risk.** The silent film consumes 30 of 180 seconds. Keep it only if it earns the serious-product reveal and resolves to the title on cue. The first spoken section should reveal the ordinary phone camera by its end; the Gold toggle should deliver the laugh. If rehearsal overruns, shorten the historical montage, not the demo or free-exit clarification.

The script's closing is the tightest slot: it currently asks for several privacy, fictional-download, deletion-fee, and thesis lines in 35 seconds. Cut repeated explanations and rehearse with the actual slide changes. Aim for about 45–55 spoken words per 30-second slot, 65–75 per 45-second slot, and leave a beat for the mode toggle and transitions. Four speakers can fit if each starts on the cue and the next slide is ready; assign one person to advance slides without adding a fifth voice.

## Focused three-minute arc

| Time | Beat and job |
|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | Silent monochrome reflection-to-phone film. Reveal THE MIRROR by 00:25; hold the title for the handoff. |
| 00:30–01:00 | Serious premium reveal, then the screen-is-the-glass line. Establish the ordinary front camera. |
| 01:00–01:45 | Demonstrate Silver partial screen → Gold full screen. Land “same camera, more expensive empty space.” State the local, temporary preview and show fallback if needed. |
| 01:45–02:25 | Read the mock tiers and one or two extras. Label prices fictional before interaction; show the fictional download/update claims and no-purchase label. |
| 02:25–03:00 | Briefly dismiss the deletion-fee joke, show free close, then deliver the thesis. Avoid repeating all technical details. |

**Suggested spoken close (about 45 words):** “Silver is free; Gold is a mock ₹2,000 to unlock the rest of your own screen. The extras, download, updates, and deletion fee are jokes; there are no purchases, downloads, or paid exits. The camera preview stays local and temporary. We built a mirror for a phone that already had one. That’s the point.”

**Answer to “what did you build and why is it useless?”** “We built a phone app that uses the front camera as a mirror, then satirizes premium software by charging—in mock UI—for the rest of the screen and other imaginary upgrades. It is useless because the phone already has a camera and a screen.”

## Assumptions

I treated the listed runtime and product behaviors as fixed, and the screen controls, optional local camera preview, fallback, mock pricing, and free exit as implemented as described. I have not assumed any unstated judging rubric. The script should claim only behaviors the team has verified in the demo build.
