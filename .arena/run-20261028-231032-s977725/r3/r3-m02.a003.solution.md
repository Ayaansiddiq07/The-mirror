## What the team should protect

Keep the restrained monochrome reveal and the serious-to-satirical turn. The clearest joke is visual: Silver shows a partial-screen reflection, then Gold sells the remaining screen space while using the same camera. Let the switch land before explaining it.

## Likely judge questions and risk

- **“What did you actually build, and can I try it?” — High risk if the site is presented as a finished phone app.** The materials identify a presentation site with an interactive camera demo, Silver/Gold controls, mock pricing, and a free-exit joke. Call it a browser demo/prototype unless a separate phone app is available. Rehearse the camera path and the camera-denied illustration fallback.
- **“Does the camera stop when I leave, and is anything saved?” — High trust risk unless the interaction and explanation agree.** The preview is local and temporary, and the implementation context says camera tracks stop on slide changes, when the page is hidden, and on unload. However, the “CLOSE THE APP — FREE, ALWAYS” button first opens the deletion-fee dialog; that click alone does not stop the preview. The dialog’s “CLOSE FOR FREE” action does. Best fix: stop the camera when the exit dialog opens. Until then, say accurately: “Permission is optional; the preview is local and is not recorded or uploaded. Choose ‘CLOSE FOR FREE’ to end it.” Keep the illustration fallback ready.
- **“Are the prices and giant download real?” — Moderate risk if the joke is not unmistakable.** Silver is free, Gold is a mock one-time ₹2,000 upgrade, and extras are mock UI at ₹5,000. The 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months are fictional and must trigger no download or purchase. Label the extras control itself as mock, and make confirmation copy say “imaginary” or “pretended”; “activated” can briefly read as a real action. Speaker 3 should say once that all charges and download claims are fictional.
- **“Is there really one trial?” — Moderate risk if presented as implemented.** The task defines the assumed trial as a one-time first-run preview, but the reviewed script does not establish that behavior. Show it only if the build implements it; otherwise omit the trial claim.
- **“Is the demo safe and within the rules?” — High risk if any prohibited behavior is present.** The task forbids device locking, personal-data collection, rapid flashing, and loud audio. Before presenting, verify that the app never locks the device or retains personal data, and review the opening film and transitions for rapid flashes and excessive sound. The script already calls for a silent film or restrained room tone and gentle, non-explosive motion; keep those choices, and inspect the actual cut for rapid flashing. State briefly that the camera permission is optional and the preview stays local and temporary. This check is distinct from explaining privacy.
- **“Does the film earn 30 seconds, and can four speakers fit?” — Moderate risk.** The assigned flow requires a 30-second silent opening and it establishes the serious reveal. Preserve it, rehearse the title reveal and handoffs, and avoid repeating pricing, download, and privacy disclaimers in the close. The spoken sections are brisk; time them aloud with the live toggles and pauses, trimming feature-list wording first if needed.

The ₹2,000 deletion fee is a dismissible joke, and closing must always be free. Keep the free route obvious and ensure the camera stops when that route is selected; ideally stop it as soon as the exit dialog opens.

## Focused three-minute pitch

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film:** water reflection → mirror history → layered phone → title at 00:25. Use gentle transitions, no rapid flashes, and silence or restrained room tone.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal:** deliver the serious premium introduction, ending: “The glass is conveniently the screen you already own.”
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo:** show Silver, switch to Gold, and say: “Same camera, same person, same phone; Gold just sells the empty space.” Explain briefly that permission is optional and the preview is local and temporary. If permission is denied, show the illustration and continue. Confirm the camera stops on exit.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing:** explain free Silver, mock one-time ₹2,000 Gold, and mock ₹5,000 extras. Point to the fictional-download disclaimer and say there is no purchase or download. Do not claim a trial unless it is demonstrably implemented.
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close:** say what the team built, why it is useless, then thank the judges. Avoid repeating disclaimers already covered.

## Short answer: what did you build and why is it useless?

“We built a browser demo of a front-camera mirror with Silver and Gold modes, mock premium prices, and a free exit. It is useless because your phone already has the camera and screen to do the same thing; the app sells the empty space as an upgrade.”

**Suggested closing line:** “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the point of useless software. Thank you.”
