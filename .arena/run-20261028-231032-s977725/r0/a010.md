# The Mirror — pitch review

The concept is strongest when the demo proves the joke: Silver shows half a screen, Gold reveals the same reflection across the whole display, and the “upgrade” sells the empty space. The premium monochrome reveal supports that turn. The script already makes the safety boundaries clear; tighten the spoken version so they sound like part of the build, not a disclaimer intermission.

## Likely judge objections and fixes

- **“Is this just a camera preview with pricing jokes?” — Real risk.** The closing answers this, but arrives late. State the build plainly at the demo’s start: “We built a local front-camera mirror with two display modes: Silver uses part of the screen; Gold uses all of it.” Then let the visual and price reveal carry the satire.
- **“Does it actually work, and what happens if the camera fails?” — Real risk for a live demo.** Permission may be denied, unavailable, or slow. Lead with the local illustration already visible; request camera access only on the deliberate tap, and use a rehearsed fallback line: “Camera is optional; this local illustration shows the same Silver-to-Gold change.” Don’t spend pitch time troubleshooting.
- **“Are you collecting or storing camera data?” — Real trust risk.** The script says “nothing is recorded or saved,” but does not say “uploaded” until later. At the demo, say once: “The preview stays on this device temporarily; we don’t record, upload, or retain camera data.” Keep it short and consistent with the implementation.
- **“Are those fees and download claims real?” — Real clarity risk.** The 25 GB / 10 GB claims, ₹2,000 deletion fee, and ₹5,000 extras can read as actual asks if the screen is glanced at mid-pitch. Put “FICTIONAL — NO DOWNLOADS OR PURCHASES” beside the download claim and mock prices; keep “CLOSE FOR FREE” prominent. Say the upgrade is mock UI before any button interaction. Make clear the one-time preview means first run, not a recurring trial.
- **“Does the 30-second silent film earn its time?” — A pacing risk, not a concept flaw.** It consumes one sixth of the pitch before the product is named. Keep the specified film, but rehearse its start and title cue so it lands exactly at :30; don’t let the film or transitions steal time from the actual demo. Keep the first spoken reveal straight-faced; save the unmistakable joke for Silver/Gold.
- **“Can four people finish in three minutes?” — Manageable with rehearsal.** The current close repeats privacy, fictional file sizes, and the deletion gag after pricing has established them. Remove those repeats. Give speakers fixed handoff cues and rehearse the camera-denied path; preserve the final sentence if time slips.

## Focused three-minute arc

| Time | Beat and purpose |
|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | Silent film: mirror history, engineered phone reveal, title at :25. Speaker 1 starts on title. |
| 00:30–01:00 | Serious reveal: front camera, polished mirror experience; hold back the full joke. |
| 01:00–01:45 | Speaker 2 demonstrates Silver then Gold. State the local/temporary/no-recording privacy line once; use illustration if camera is denied. |
| 01:45–02:25 | Speaker 3: Silver free; Gold mock ₹2,000; ₹5,000 absurd extras; label all prices and 25 GB / 10 GB claims fictional, with no transactions or downloads. Keep deletion-fee gag dismissible and exit free. |
| 02:25–03:00 | Speaker 4 gives the point in one or two sentences, then thanks the audience. No new feature list. |

**Short answer:** “We built a temporary, local front-camera mirror with a partial-screen Silver mode and full-screen Gold mode. It’s useless because Gold charges, as a joke, to use the rest of a screen for the same reflection.”

**Suggested closing:** “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the whole point of useless software. Thank you.” Cut the repeated safety and file-size explanation from the close; those details have already landed where they matter.

## Assumptions

I treat the listed 00:00–03:00 flow and 30-second silent film as fixed constraints, and “one trial” as a one-time first-run preview. I’m reviewing the supplied script and presentation context, not assuming an unstated scoring rubric or independently certifying implementation behavior.
