# The Mirror — pitch review

## Assessment

The core joke is clear: a phone already has a front camera, while The Mirror sells its owner screen area and imaginary premium value. Silver and Gold make that joke visible in a demo. Keep the monochrome, serious-launch delivery and let the product claims and prices reveal the satire.

## Likely judge objections

- **“What did you actually build?” — genuinely risky.** The script makes the proposition clear but should make the working app unmistakable. During the demo, show the actual front-camera preview when permission is granted, visibly switch from Silver’s partial-screen view to Gold’s full-screen view, and name them as working modes. If permission is denied or unavailable, identify the illustration as a fallback; do not describe it as live camera footage.
- **“Is the camera recording or sending anything?” — genuinely risky because this is a trust question.** The script says nothing is recorded or saved, while the on-screen note gives the fuller assurance. Say it plainly during the demo: “Camera permission is optional. The preview stays on this device while the demo is open; we don’t record, upload, or retain it.” Show the fallback, and do not treat a permission prompt as proof of privacy.
- **“Are the prices and download claims real?” — risky if the disclosures are missed.** The script already says the upgrade buttons are jokes and, in the closing, explicitly calls the 25 GB download and 10 GB updates fictional. Repeat “fictional” as the download/update claims appear so the disclosure lands beside the UI. Label the ₹5,000 extras bundle as mock-only too. Make the ₹2,000 deletion-fee joke visibly dismissible, with a free exit.
- **“Why spend 30 seconds on a film?” — a real trade-off, not a fatal flaw.** The silent film takes one-sixth of the pitch and postpones proof that the app runs. Keep it because the serious-to-satirical reveal is part of the brief, but let THE MIRROR appear at 00:25 and use the last five seconds to resolve on a recognizable phone/app image. Do not extend the slot.
- **“Can four speakers fit the clock?” — genuinely risky.** The closing is about 78 words for a 35-second slot, before pauses, handoff time, or laughter. The demo also needs time for two mode changes. Rehearse with the fixed clock, and trim feature-list wording before the product proof or privacy and mock-only clarifications.

The “AI-ENHANCED YOU” and “4K” graphics could sound like real capabilities. Keep them visibly tongue-in-cheek or label them as mock claims, so Q&A stays on the actual app and the joke.

## Focused three-minute pitch

| Time | Speaker | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Water-to-phone film; title lands at 00:25. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Serious reveal; establish that this is a working front-camera mirror, then puncture the “engineered glass” claim. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Show Silver, switch to Gold, state the same camera fills more screen, and give the privacy line and fallback. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Silver free, Gold ₹2,000, extras ₹5,000. Call the extras and download/update claims fictional or mock-only as they appear; land one or two pricing gags. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | State what was built and why it is useless, deliver the dismissible deletion-fee/free-exit gag, and close. Aim for about 50 spoken words to leave room for the final beat. |

Suggested close (51 words):

“We built a working front-camera mirror with two modes: Silver shows part of the screen; Gold fills it. The preview stays on-device and is never recorded or uploaded. The prices, download sizes, and deletion fee are jokes; leaving is free. We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. Thank you.”

Keep the handoff cues visible to speakers. If rehearsal runs long, shorten the confidence-filter and extras patter first; preserve the demo, privacy assurance, explicit fiction disclosures, and free-exit line.

## Assumptions

This review is based on the supplied pitch script and implementation description, not a live rehearsal or runtime inspection. It assumes the camera preview, fallback, mode controls, and mock-only safeguards behave as described. Rehearse both camera-permitted and camera-denied paths before relying on the demo. No unstated judging rubric is assumed.
