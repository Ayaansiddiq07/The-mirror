## Review

The Mirror’s strongest joke is simple: it sells the screen space that a phone already uses as a mirror. Keep the premium monochrome reveal, then let the Silver-to-Gold switch demonstrate the absurdity. The main risks are whether judges see a working app, whether camera and payment claims are trustworthy, and whether the satire is clear without being explained twice. Four reviewers’ priorities converge here: show the product early, make safety and fiction explicit in one clean pass, and preserve the exact assigned timing windows by trimming lines inside them.

## Likely judge objections and practical fixes

- **“Is this actually built and demoable?” — Highest execution risk.** Show the app itself: toggle Silver’s partial-screen reflection to Gold’s full-screen reflection. The same person and camera feed make the joke legible. If permission is denied or the camera is unavailable, use the local illustration and identify it as the fallback; do not imply it is live video.
- **“What does the app do with camera data?” — High trust risk, easy to resolve.** Say once: “With permission, the preview stays on this device temporarily; we do not record, upload, or retain it. If you decline, the illustration stays on screen.” This covers the privacy promise and the camera-denied path without a long technical detour.
- **“Are the prices, fee, and download real?” — High clarity risk if the UI looks actionable.** Mark upgrade controls “mock / no payment”; say no purchase happens. Label the 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months “fictional—no download.” Call the ₹2,000 deletion fee a dismissible joke and keep “Close for free” visible. Deliver this once; do not repeat the data claims in the close.
- **“Why devote 30 seconds to a film?” — Moderate pacing risk.** The silent film supports the serious reveal, so keep it, but make it a quick visual progression from reflection to phone. Reveal the title near 00:25 and begin the product line immediately at 00:30.
- **“Can four people fit the pitch into three minutes?” — Moderate rehearsal risk.** The fixed slots total exactly three minutes, with no extra transition time. Keep the listed windows unchanged, rehearse with a hard clock, and trim feature lists or pauses if needed. Do not cut the privacy, no-payment, free-exit, or final thesis lines.

## Short answer to the core question

**What did you build and why is it useless?** “We built a phone mirror using the front camera, with a partial-screen Silver mode and full-screen Gold mode. It is useless because the phone already has a front camera—and we pretend the empty screen space is a premium upgrade.”

## Assumptions

The four speakers and five time windows are fixed; the film is required. The stated Silver/Gold controls, camera fallback, mock prices, and free exit are implemented as described. Prices, download claims, and the deletion fee are fictional satire. No unstated judging rubric is assumed.
