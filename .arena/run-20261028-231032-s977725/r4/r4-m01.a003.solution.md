# The Mirror — judge-proof pitch review

## Outline

1. Protect the serious premium reveal and the visual Silver-to-Gold joke.
2. Identify likely judge questions, rank the genuine risks, and tie findings to the supplied script and demo implementation.
3. Recommend practical fixes that preserve the satire and satisfy the stated safety constraints.
4. Give the timed four-speaker arc, a concise “what did you build and why is it useless?” answer, and assumptions.

## What to protect

Keep the restrained monochrome reveal and the serious-to-satirical turn. The strongest joke is visible without explanation: Silver shows a partial-screen reflection, then Gold sells the remaining screen space while using the same camera. Let the switch land before describing it.

## Likely judge questions and recommended fixes

- **“What did you actually build, and can I try it?” — High risk if described as a finished phone app.** The supplied materials identify a presentation-site demo with Silver/Gold controls, mock pricing, and a camera interaction. Call it a browser demo/prototype unless a separate phone app is available. Rehearse both the live camera path and the camera-denied illustration fallback.

- **“Does the camera stop when I leave, and is anything saved?” — High trust risk until the permission race is fixed and checked.** The implementation calls `stopCamera()` on slide changes, when the document becomes hidden, and on unload. But if permission is pending during navigation, `stopCamera()` sees no stream; a later `getUserMedia()` resolution can assign a live stream after leaving the demo slide. After permission resolves, check that the demo slide is still active and the document visible; if not, immediately stop every track on the returned stream and do not attach it. Also stop the stream when the exit dialog opens. Test navigation and hiding the page while permission is pending, then grant permission. Until that is fixed and verified, avoid saying the current handlers guarantee that leaving stops the camera. A safe spoken line is: “Camera permission is optional. The preview is local and is not recorded or uploaded; we’ve tested that leaving the demo stops the camera.” Use that last clause only after the race test passes. Keep the illustration fallback ready.

- **“Are the prices and giant download real?” — Moderate risk if the joke is not unmistakable.** State once that the ₹2,000 Gold upgrade, ₹5,000 extras, 25 GB download, and 10 GB updates every two months are fictional. The download claims must remain mock UI and trigger no download or purchase. Label the extras control as mock and use “imaginary” or “pretend” in confirmation copy so “activated” cannot briefly sound real.

- **“Is there really one trial?” — Moderate risk if claimed as implemented.** The task defines the assumed trial as a one-time first-run preview, but the supplied script does not establish that behavior. Mention it only if the build demonstrably implements it; otherwise omit it.

- **“Is the demo safe and within the rules?” — High risk if prohibited behavior remains.** The task forbids device locking, personal-data collection, rapid flashing, and loud audio. Before presenting, verify that the app never locks the device or retains personal data, and inspect the actual film and transitions for rapid flashes and loud sound. The script already proposes silence or restrained room tone and gentle motion; retain those choices. Explain briefly that permission is optional and that preview data is not recorded, uploaded, or retained. Check the pending-permission race above as part of the camera safety review.

- **“Does the film earn 30 seconds, and can four speakers fit?” — Moderate delivery risk.** The assigned flow reserves a 30-second silent opening and it establishes the serious reveal, so preserve it. Rehearse the title reveal, UI toggles, and handoffs with a timer. The spoken sections are brisk; trim feature-list wording first if the run is long, and avoid repeating privacy, pricing, and download disclaimers in the close.

The ₹2,000 deletion fee is a dismissible joke, and closing must always be free. Keep the free route obvious. Stop any active camera stream when the exit dialog opens and again when free exit is selected.

## Focused three-minute pitch

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film:** Water reflection → mirror history → layered phone → title at 00:25. Use gentle transitions, no rapid flashes, and silence or restrained room tone.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal:** Deliver the serious premium introduction, ending: “The glass is conveniently the screen you already own.”
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo:** Show Silver, switch to Gold, then say: “Same camera, same person, same phone; Gold just sells the empty space.” Explain that permission is optional and the preview stays local, with no recording or upload. If permission is denied, use the illustration and continue. Say the camera stops on leaving only after the pending-permission race has been fixed and tested.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing:** Explain free Silver, mock one-time ₹2,000 Gold, and mock ₹5,000 extras. Say once that all prices and download claims are fictional and trigger no transaction or download. Do not claim a trial unless the build demonstrates it.
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close:** State what the team built and why it is useless, then thank the judges. Avoid repeating disclaimers already covered.

## Short answer: what did you build and why is it useless?

“We built a browser demo of a front-camera mirror with Silver and Gold modes, mock premium prices, and a free exit. It is useless because your phone already has the camera and screen to do the same thing; the app sells the empty space as an upgrade.”

**Suggested closing line:** “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the point of useless software. Thank you.”
