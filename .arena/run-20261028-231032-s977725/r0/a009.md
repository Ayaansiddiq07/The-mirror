# The Mirror — pitch review

The premise is strongest when the audience first recognizes the language of a premium launch, then sees that the “upgrade” only reveals screen space they already had. Keep the restrained monochrome reveal and let the demo deliver that turn. The concept is easy to understand; the main execution risk is spending too long explaining each joke after it has landed.

## Likely judge objections, ranked by risk

1. **“Is this a working app, or just a polished deck?” — High risk.** The script promises a live Silver/Gold toggle and optional camera preview, but spends little spoken time establishing what is actually implemented. Open the demo by toggling the real controls immediately. Name the working parts plainly: partial-screen mirror, full-screen mirror, fallback illustration. Avoid calling mock pricing or visual extras functional features. If the camera prompt delays the demo, use the fallback and keep going.

2. **“Does the camera access or retain anything?” — High trust risk if vague; low if the implementation matches the claim.** “Local,” “temporary,” “nothing recorded or saved,” and “no retained personal data” are useful assurances, but repeated versions consume precious time and sound like guarantees the team must be able to substantiate. Say it once during the demo: “The preview stays on this device for the session; we don’t record, upload, or retain it. Camera access is optional.” Ensure this describes the actual implementation, including any analytics or logs. Keep the visible permission-denied fallback.

3. **“Are these prices and download sizes real?” — Medium risk.** The ₹2,000 Gold upgrade, ₹5,000 extras, 25 GB download, recurring 10 GB updates, and ₹2,000 deletion fee are deliberately fictional, but a realistic checkout or download affordance could confuse the joke. Put “MOCK ONLY · NO PURCHASE OR DOWNLOAD” beside the pricing and download claims, and make the buttons show a joke confirmation only. Say that once aloud. Make the deletion fee easy to dismiss and keep “CLOSE FOR FREE, ALWAYS” visible at the exit. The script already states these safeguards; the UI should make them equally clear.

4. **“Is the opening film worth 30 seconds?” — Medium, contingent on the film.** It uses one-sixth of the pitch before anyone speaks. It can earn that time if it is a brisk, silent premium reveal that makes the product feel serious and reaches the title on cue. The concept’s water-to-phone history risks feeling like generic launch filler. Keep only the visual beats that build anticipation; have the next line immediately puncture the reveal. If the film overruns or the title lands late, shorten it rather than borrowing time from the live demo.

5. **“Can four speakers finish cleanly in three minutes?” — Medium.** The allocations total exactly 3:00, leaving no recovery time. The spoken copy is roughly 270 words, which is feasible at a measured pace, but demo interaction, slide changes, laughter, or camera permission can eat the buffer. Rehearse with transitions and toggles included. Speaker 3’s feature list and Speaker 4’s repeated disclaimers are the easiest trims. Preserve one clear privacy line, one clear no-purchase line, and the final point.

## Recommended three-minute arc

| Time | Speaker | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Premium visual reveal; title lands by 00:25. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Introduce the ordinary front-camera mirror with polished launch language; end on “the screen you already own.” |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Toggle Silver to Gold; point out that only the screen area changes. State the camera/privacy and fallback behavior once. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Deliver ₹2,000 Gold, ₹5,000 extras, and one or two best gags. Label the UI fictional; mention fictional download sizes briefly, not as another feature list. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | Close with the fake deletion-fee joke, immediately show free dismissal, then land the core idea. Do not re-explain every pricing and privacy detail. |

Use a hard cue for each handoff and rehearse the actual controls. The one-trial preview should be a single first-run preview; don’t imply recurring free trials. Keep all fees, upgrades, and download claims visibly fictional. No part of the gag should lock the device, collect personal data, flash rapidly, or use loud audio.

## Suggested answer to “What did you build, and why is it useless?”

“We built a phone mirror using the front camera, with a partial-screen Silver mode and a full-screen Gold mode. The camera preview is temporary and local. It’s useless because Gold charges ₹2,000 to show you the screen space your phone already had.”

This gives judges the artifact, its visible behavior, and the joke in three short sentences. If the implementation cannot substantiate “temporary and local,” describe only the verified behavior.
