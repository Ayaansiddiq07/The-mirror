# The Mirror: pitch review

## Likely judge objections and practical fixes

1. **Will the demo work, and is it genuinely built?** The app’s simple premise makes a broken permission prompt or missed mode change especially visible. Rehearse Silver-to-Gold once, request camera access only at the demo, and have the camera-denied illustration ready. Show that Silver is partial-screen and Gold is full-screen; say the preview is temporary and local, with no recording, upload, or retention.

2. **Could the joke look like a real charge or download?** Put “mock” or “fictional” beside every price and the 25 GB / 10 GB claims. Say “There is no checkout” before showing prices. Make clear the download and updates trigger no files, and that “one trial” means a one-time first-run preview.

3. **Could the deletion gag feel coercive?** Keep the ₹2,000 fee visibly dismissible and label it as a joke. Do not make it a required demo action. Show “Close for free” clearly and end the pricing bit with that option.

4. **Does the satire land without crowding the close?** The 30-second film is a fixed part of the three-minute slot, so keep it intact. Let it reveal the actual app and title by 00:25. Trim spoken feature lists and leave the close enough space for the free-exit action and a final pause.

## Recommended pitch arc

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film:** Water, polished metal, glass, phone. Reveal the actual app and title by 00:25, then hold the reveal for five seconds.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, product reveal:** Present the polished “reflection experience” seriously, then reveal the joke with the screen-you-already-own line.
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo:** Show Silver partial-screen, then Gold full-screen. Point out that the reflection and camera are unchanged. If permission is denied or unavailable, use the ready illustration and continue.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing:** Start with “These are mock prices; there is no checkout.” Show ₹2,000 Gold and ₹5,000 extras, then one absurd fictional download/update claim. If shown, dismiss the deletion-fee joke and visibly choose free exit.
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close:** Keep the privacy explanation brief, identify the fictional claims and free exit, and finish on why a mirror app is useless. Rehearse the whole run with mode changes and pauses against the three-minute clock.

## Short answer

“We built a phone app that shows a temporary, local front-camera mirror: Silver uses part of the screen, while Gold uses all of it. It is useless because the phone already had a camera and a screen, and our premium upgrade charges for using more of the screen.”

## Suggested closing

“We built a temporary, local front-camera mirror. It records and uploads nothing. The giant download, updates, and deletion fee are jokes; closing is free. We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the point of useless software.”

## Assumptions

The five time blocks and the silent 30-second film are fixed. “One trial” is the one-time first-run preview. The brief supplies the camera privacy and fallback behavior; I have not independently inspected the implementation.
