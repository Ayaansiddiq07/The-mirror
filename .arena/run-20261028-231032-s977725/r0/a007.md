# The Mirror pitch review

## Strongest likely objections

- **“Is there a real, demoable product here?” — genuinely risky if the pitch only describes the joke.** Show the actual Silver-to-Gold change on screen, then show the camera fallback if permission is denied. Keep the explanation concrete: “Silver shows the local front-camera preview in part of the screen; Gold expands that same preview to the full screen.” Avoid implying that the mock extras or download claims are implemented.
- **“What happens to camera data?” — genuinely risky because camera access invites the question.** Answer at the demo, beside the permission control: “Camera access is optional. The preview stays on this device temporarily; we do not record, upload, or retain it.” Rehearse the denied-permission path so the demo still works without access.
- **“Are these charges or download claims real?” — genuinely risky if a judge could mistake the interface for a transaction.** Put “MOCK ONLY · NO PURCHASE” on both the ₹2,000 Gold and ₹5,000 extras actions, not just in narration. Label the 25 GB download and 10 GB updates as fictional beside the claims. Keep the deletion-fee dialog dismissible and put “CLOSE FOR FREE” at least as visibly as the fee.
- **“Does the film earn its 30 seconds?” — a real pacing risk, not a flaw in the premise.** Thirty seconds is one-sixth of the pitch. Retain the serious premium reveal, but have the title land by 00:25 and make the phone unmistakable before the spoken product reveal. If rehearsal runs long, shorten the montage rather than the demo or privacy clarification.
- **“Will the ending fit?” — genuinely risky in rehearsal.** The current close stacks the product explanation, privacy, fake download sizes, deletion fee, free exit, and thesis into 35 seconds. That is too much to deliver cleanly while landing the final line. Move the download and fee gags into the pricing segment and reserve the close for the point.

The serious-to-satirical turn is working: “the glass…is conveniently the screen you already own” starts the reveal, and “charged the empty space with ambition” gives the demo its strongest punchline. Keep those. The monochrome premium treatment supports the joke. The visible “AI-ENHANCED YOU” and “YOUR FACE, IN 4K” labels could read as real claims; make their absurdity evident in context or mark them as mock product language.

## Focused three-minute arc

| Time | Speaker | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Serious reflection-to-phone film; title by 00:25. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Reveal that the “advanced glass” is the existing screen; let the joke emerge without explaining it twice. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Demonstrate Silver, switch to Gold, land “same reflection, more ambition”; give the privacy sentence and use the illustration if camera access is denied. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Silver free, Gold ₹2,000 once, extras ₹5,000; make clear every purchase is mock. Deliver the fictional 25 GB / 10 GB claims and deletion-fee joke quickly, with the free exit visible. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | State what was built, why it is useless, and finish on the thesis. No new feature list. |

Use one speaker per spoken segment, with Speaker 1 carrying the opening through the reveal. The demo speaker should operate the mode controls while speaking; the next speaker can advance the deck during the handoff. Rehearse to the listed cutoffs, especially the 45-second demo and the final 35 seconds.

## Short answer: what did you build, and why is it useless?

“We built a phone app that turns its front camera into a mirror, then sells the same reflection back in premium tiers. Silver uses part of the screen; Gold uses all of it. The camera preview is temporary and local. The upgrades and fees are jokes, and closing is always free. It is useless because the phone already had a mirror.”

## Assumptions

I treat the three-minute segment timings as fixed and the camera behavior described in the brief as the intended implementation. The review makes no assumptions about unstated judging criteria.
