# The Mirror — judge-proof pitch review

## Strongest likely judge objections

1. **Is the app genuinely built and demoable? — High risk if the live interaction fails.** The pitch promises a Silver/Gold demo, and the presentation includes both controls plus a camera-denied illustration fallback. Rehearse the actual build: show Silver partial-screen, switch to Gold full-screen, and use the fallback promptly if permission is denied or unavailable. Describe the premium extras and fees as mock UI, not implemented paid features.

2. **What happens to the camera feed? — High trust risk unless verified.** The pitch says the preview is temporary and local, with no recording, upload, or retained personal data. Confirm this behavior in the running build before stating it. If confirmed, say it once plainly during the demo; keep camera use optional and the denied-permission fallback usable. If not confirmed, do not make the claim until corrected.

3. **Are the prices and download claims real? — Medium risk.** The ₹2,000 Gold price and ₹5,000 extras should be unmistakably mock pricing; no payment occurs. Label the 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months as fictional, and ensure they trigger no downloads. The ₹2,000 deletion fee is a dismissible joke; state that closing is always free. Keep the fictional labels readable when those claims appear.

4. **Does the 30-second film earn its time? — Medium, execution-dependent.** It takes one-sixth of the pitch before the first spoken line. Keep the serious monochrome tone and serious-to-satirical reveal, but make each visual beat progress toward the product and land THE MIRROR at 00:25. Rehearse the title cue; trim montage beats if the reveal or demo starts late.

5. **Can four speakers fit three minutes? — Medium.** The time blocks fill the full slot, leaving little room for handoffs, interface delay, or laughter. Rehearse with the actual deck and a timer. Trim the forecast and extra-feature list first; protect the reveal, mode switch, one verified privacy sentence, explicit mock/no-payment clarification, and crisp closing line.

These are likely practical questions arising from the brief, not claims about an unstated judging rubric. This review does not independently verify the running app, privacy behavior, film, or spoken timing.

## Focused pitch structure

| Time | Speaker | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Premium monochrome film; title at 00:25. No loud audio or rapid flashing. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Serious product reveal; end on the “glass” being the screen already owned. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Demo Silver then Gold; give one privacy sentence only if verified. Use the camera-denied fallback without waiting. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Mock ₹2,000 and ₹5,000 prices, one or two extras jokes, and a clear no-payment/no-download clarification. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | Dismissible deletion-fee joke, free exit, then the point of the app in one crisp line. |

## Suggested spoken wording

**Reveal (00:30–01:00):** “Introducing The Mirror: an elegant reflection experience, powered by the front camera already on your phone. We engineered the glass too. Conveniently, it is the screen you already own.”

**Demo (01:00–01:45):** “Silver gives your reflection a tasteful partial-screen window. Gold expands it to the full screen. Same camera, same reflection; we have simply put a price on the empty space. Camera access is optional. The preview stays on this device for this session; we do not record, upload, or retain it.” Use the last two sentences only after checking the running build. If permission is denied, show the fallback and continue.

**Pricing (01:45–02:25):** “Silver is free. Gold mock-prices the rest of your screen at a one-time ₹2,000. For ₹5,000, the extras bundle includes a confidence filter, unlimited angles—which is still one angle—and a cloth for the glass that was already there. These are fictional prices; no payment happens. The 25 GB download and 10 GB updates are fictional too; nothing is downloaded.” If the team needs time, show the size claims in clearly labeled mock UI rather than spending spoken time listing them.

**Close (02:25–03:00):** “There is even a fake ₹2,000 deletion fee. Dismiss it and close for free, always. We built a phone mirror that mock-prices screen space your phone already had. That is why it is useless. Thank you.”

## Direct answer: what did you build, and why is it useless?

“We built a phone mirror using the front camera, with a partial-screen Silver mode and a full-screen Gold mode. Camera access is optional, and the preview is temporary, with a fallback if access is denied. Gold mock-prices the extra screen space at ₹2,000; no payment occurs. It is useless because it charges in the joke to reveal screen space the phone already had.”

Only state that preview data stays local and is not recorded, uploaded, or retained after verifying the behavior in the running app.

2. **Satirical premise and serious-to-satirical reveal:** Preserved in the film, reveal wording, Silver/Gold demo, mock pricing, and closing joke.
3. **Four speakers and three-minute timing, including silent 30-second opening:** Mapped in the schedule; rehearsal advice identifies what to trim.
4. **Short answer to what was built and why it is useless:** Given directly above, with the ₹2,000 charge explicitly described as mock pricing and no payment.
5. **Assumptions stated:** The app, privacy behavior, film, and timing are not independently verified; privacy wording is conditional on checking the build.
6. **Specified product and safety behavior:** Includes one-time mock Gold upgrade, mock extras, fictional non-triggering download claims, optional temporary local preview, camera-denied fallback, no personal-data retention, dismissible deletion joke, always-free closing, and no device locking, rapid flashing, or loud audio.
