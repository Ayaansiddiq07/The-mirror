# The Mirror: judge-proof pitch review

## Likely judge objections and practical fixes

| Objection | Risk | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| “Is this a real, working app or just a launch page?” | High: the pitch promises a live demo. | Rehearse the exact presentation device and browser. Begin with Silver ready; request camera permission only after an intentional tap. If access is denied or unavailable, say “Camera access is optional; this illustration is our fallback” and demonstrate the Silver/Gold controls with it. |
| “Are the prices, download, and deletion fee real?” | High if the audience has to infer the answer. | Show “MOCK ONLY · NO PURCHASE OR DOWNLOAD” beside pricing and fictional specs when they appear. Say clearly that Gold is a mock one-time ₹2,000 upgrade, extras are mock UI at ₹5,000, the 25 GB download and 10 GB updates are fictional and trigger no downloads, the deletion fee is dismissible, and closing is always free. |
| “What happens to the camera preview?” | High enough to answer plainly; trust should not depend on the joke. | Say once during the demo: “The preview stays on this device temporarily; we do not record, upload, or retain it.” Keep the camera-denied fallback visible. |
| “Does the joke arrive too late?” | Moderate. A silent 30-second opener takes one-sixth of the pitch. | Keep the serious-to-satirical reveal, but make the film a brisk visual progression and land THE MIRROR title at 00:25. At 00:30, turn with “the glass is the screen you already own.” Do not add narration or more history. |
| “Can four speakers fit three minutes, and does the demo follow the rules?” | Moderate: live toggles and transitions take time; explicit safety constraints matter. | Rehearse to the caps with a timer and speakers ready at each handoff. Before the demo, verify it never locks the device, contains no rapid flashing, and has no loud audio. Trim feature lists before the privacy sentence, mock-only clarification, or final point. |

The product-launch keynote is a useful analogy: a short trailer establishes the premium mood, while the live demo proves the product and the ending explains the absurdity. It breaks at the stakes: this app must make fictional fees and real camera behavior unmistakable, and it must comply with the stated device and presentation constraints. Treat those as product facts, not fine print.

## Recommended three-minute arc

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film.** Water → polished metal → glass → phone. Reveal the title at 00:25; no narration or loud audio.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal.** One premium-sounding sentence, then the reveal that the “glass” is the existing screen. Avoid unverified performance claims such as “instant.”
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo.** Show Silver partial-screen, switch to Gold full-screen, land the same-camera/same-person joke. Give the short privacy line and use the illustration if permission is denied.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing.** Silver ₹0; Gold mock one-time ₹2,000; extras mock UI ₹5,000. Mark the fictional specs and no-purchase/no-download status on screen as they appear.
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close.** Confirm free exit, then explain the point. Leave a beat before “Thank you.”

## Tighter spoken version

**00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1**  
“Introducing The Mirror: a premium reflection experience, powered by the front camera already in your phone. Thoughtfully designed, with every detail considered—including the glass, which is conveniently the screen you already own.”

**01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2**  
“Silver gives your reflection a tasteful half-screen window, leaving the other half for nothing. Gold expands it to the whole screen. Same camera, same person, same phone; we have simply given the empty space ambition. Camera access is optional. The preview stays on this device temporarily: no recording, upload, or retention. If access is denied, this illustration is our fallback.”

**01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3**  
“Silver is free: half a screen, all the reflection. Gold is a mock one-time ₹2,000 to unlock the rest of your own screen. The mock ₹5,000 extras add a confidence filter, unlimited angles—which is one angle—and a cloth for the glass that was already there. No purchase happens. Our fictional spec sheet claims a 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months; no files download. The forecast is imaginary; the margins are excellent.”

**02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4**  
“We built The Mirror to show how a basic feature can be dressed up as a premium product. The camera preview is temporary and local; we do not record, upload, or retain it. There is even a fake ₹2,000 deletion fee: dismiss it and closing stays free. We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the whole point of useless software. Thank you.”

The spoken copy is about 180 words after the film, leaving time for toggles and pauses if delivered clearly. Keep Gold and extras visibly marked “MOCK ONLY”; the current page marks Gold but the extras control reads “ADD PREMIUM EXTRAS.” Put “FICTIONAL · NO DOWNLOAD” beside the specs and show free exit where the deletion joke appears. Confirm the demo remains unlocked and contains no rapid flashing or loud audio.

## Short answer for judges

“We built a phone app that uses the front camera as a local, temporary mirror, then satirizes premium software with mock UI for half-screen versus full-screen reflection and imaginary extras. It is useless because the phone already has the camera and screen; the joke is dressing an ordinary feature up as a premium product.”
