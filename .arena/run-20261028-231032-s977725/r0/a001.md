# The Mirror: judge-proof pitch review

## Likely judge objections

- **“Is this actually built?” High risk if the demo is oversold.** Call it an offline browser prototype, then show the working Silver/Gold toggle and optional camera preview. Don’t imply native-phone engineering or a proprietary camera. If camera permission fails, use the illustration and keep going.
- **“Is the privacy claim true?” High trust risk if vague.** State: camera is opt-in; preview is temporary and stays on-device; it stops when switched off or the demo closes. Don’t claim “zero data” broadly; say no video or personal data is retained.
- **“Are the prices, download, and fee real?” Manageable, but label the gag before it can read as coercion.** Keep “FICTIONAL · NO PURCHASE · NO DOWNLOAD” visible on pricing UI. Call 25 GB / 10 GB claims mock UI, make the deletion-fee joke dismissible, and say exit is free when the fee appears—not only in the closing.
- **“Does the joke land without over-explaining?” Moderate risk.** The film earns a serious reveal, but “instant, ultra-clear” and “engineered every detail” postpone the turn. Pivot sooner: “The glass is your existing screen.” Let the half-screen demo and paid full-screen upgrade deliver the joke; trim repeated caveats at the end.
- **“Can four speakers fit?” Moderate risk.** There are 150 seconds for speech, transitions, demo actions, and pauses. Rehearse handoffs with a hard timer; cut feature lists first, while preserving no-purchase/free-exit clarity and the final point.

## Recommended arc (3:00 total)

| Time | Speaker | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:30 | 1, silent | Keep the monochrome film and serious reveal. Rehearse the handoff; have a ready-to-advance cue or still fallback so playback trouble doesn’t eat demo time. Thirty seconds is a costly opening. |
| 0:30–1:00 | 1 | One premium-sounding setup, then reveal that the screen already is the mirror. Identify the build as an offline browser prototype. |
| 1:00–1:45 | 2 | Toggle Silver to Gold; show the same reflection at half and full screen. Camera is optional; keep the local illustration ready. Use one short privacy line. |
| 1:45–2:25 | 3 | Land ₹0, ₹2,000, ₹5,000 and let “unlimited angles (one)” breathe. Mark pricing and 25 GB / 10 GB claims as fictional. Show the dismissible fee only if timing permits; point to free exit. |
| 2:25–3:00 | 4 | State the point once, then close. Drop repeated feature and disclaimer lists. |

Aim for **230–250 spoken words** across the 150 spoken seconds, leaving room for transitions and demo pauses. The current script is dense in the demo and close; trim there first.

**Answer to “what did you build and why is it useless?”** “We built an offline browser prototype that turns the front camera into a mirror, then sells the same reflection back as absurd premium tiers. It is useless because the phone already has a mirror, and the upgrades add no real capability.”

**Assumptions.** The presented build is the browser demo described in the project materials. Camera is optional and local; prices, trial, download/update claims, and deletion fee are mock UI. The trial is one first-run preview, not a recurring prompt. No unstated judging rubric is assumed.
