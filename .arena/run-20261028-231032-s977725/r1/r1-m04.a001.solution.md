# The Mirror: pitch and concept review

## The questions to prepare for

- **“What is actually built?” High risk if the team implies more than the demo shows.** Call it a browser prototype. Demonstrate the working Silver/Gold screen change and, if available, the optional camera preview. Don’t call it offline or imply native-phone engineering; the materials do not establish either. Have the camera-denied illustration ready and continue without treating permission as a failure.
- **“What happens to camera data?” High trust risk if the answer is broad or vague.** Say precisely: camera permission is optional; the preview stays on-device and temporary; it is not recorded, uploaded, or retained. Avoid an unsupported blanket claim such as “we collect no data.”
- **“Are these charges and downloads real?” Moderate risk, easy to control.** Label the ₹2,000 Gold upgrade, ₹5,000 extras, 25 GB download, 10 GB updates every two months, and ₹2,000 deletion fee as fictional mock UI. Make clear no purchase or download occurs. The deletion-fee joke must be dismissible and closing must remain free; say so at the fee, not only at the end.
- **“Is this just an ordinary mirror with a long disclaimer?” Moderate risk.** Let the Silver-to-Gold toggle and the same reflection deliver the joke. The current script delays the turn with “instant, ultra-clear” and “engineered every detail,” then repeats privacy and fictional-download explanations in the close. Pivot sooner and say each clarification once.
- **“Does the 30-second film earn its time, and can four speakers finish?” Moderate risk.** The silent monochrome reveal supports the requested serious-to-satirical turn, but consumes one sixth of the slot. Keep it only if it lands cleanly on THE MIRROR at 00:25; rehearse exact handoffs and protect demo time. The spoken sections have 150 seconds total, including toggles and pauses, so cut feature lists before rushing the final point.

## Focused pitch structure (3:00 total)

| Time | Speaker | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Monochrome reflection-to-phone film; title reveal at 00:25. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | One premium-sounding setup, then reveal: the “glass” is the screen already owned. Identify it as a browser prototype. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Show Silver partial-screen, then Gold full-screen. Point out that it is the same reflection. One concise line covers optional permission and temporary local preview; use the illustration if denied or unavailable. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Land free Silver, mock one-time ₹2,000 Gold, and mock ₹5,000 extras. Let “unlimited angles (one)” breathe. Label the 25 GB / 10 GB claims fictional; if showing the deletion fee, make its dismissible nature and free exit immediately clear. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | State what was built and why it is useless, then thank the judges. Do not re-list every caveat. |

Rehearse with a hard three-minute timer including the silent film, handoffs, demo actions, and pauses. Target roughly 220–240 spoken words across the 150 spoken seconds; remove secondary extras first if the demo runs long.

## Short answer

“We built a browser prototype that turns the front camera into a temporary local mirror, then jokes about selling the same reflection back in premium tiers. It is useless because the phone already has a screen and a camera; Gold mostly charges for the rest of the screen.”

## Assumptions

The team can demonstrate the Silver/Gold controls in the presentation site; camera access is optional and the local illustration is the fallback. Prices, trial, download/update claims, and deletion fee are mock UI. The assumed trial is a one-time first-run preview. No claim is made that the app runs offline, and no unstated judging rubric is assumed.
