# The Mirror — judge-proof pitch review

## Read on the concept

The premise is immediately legible once Gold reveals that the “upgrade” is the same camera showing the same person. The monochrome premium reveal supports the joke, and the interface gives the team a real demo rather than a slide-only idea. The strongest ending is already present: “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one.” Let that be the final line and give it a beat.

## Likely judge objections, ranked by risk

- **“Is this actually built, and what can you show live?” High risk if the camera fails or the demo feels like a mockup.** Lead the demo with Silver-to-Gold screen change; say the camera is optional, then enable it only if ready. If denied or unavailable, keep the illustration visible and continue without apologizing. The current fallback and controls support this. Rehearse the toggle and fallback, not just the camera happy path.
- **“Is this harmless, or are you asking for access / money?” High trust risk, easy to resolve.** Say plainly: “Camera permission is optional. The preview stays on this device and is never recorded or uploaded. Every price and download is fictional; no purchase or download happens. Closing is always free.” Current script covers these facts, but scatters them across the demo and close. Put the privacy reassurance immediately after the live preview; keep the fiction/no-purchase and free-exit clarification with the pricing joke. Don’t imply “no retained personal data” as a technical achievement beyond the actual implementation.
- **“Why is the opening film 30 seconds?” Medium risk.** A silent, polished film buys the serious-product reveal, but consumes one sixth of the pitch before judges see the app. Keep the required slot only if the cut is tight and the title lands exactly at :25; use the final five seconds as a clean reveal, not more lore. If rehearsal is tight, shorten the film and give the recovered seconds to the live demo, subject to the event’s format.
- **“Are the prices, 25 GB download, and deletion fee real?” Medium risk if the joke reads as a real paywall or is overexplained.** The UI labels the download fictional and marks Gold “MOCK ONLY”; say once that prices/downloads are jokes and once that exit is free. Make the ₹2,000 deletion fee visibly dismissible, with “CLOSE FOR FREE” as the clear action. Don’t stage a fake purchase confirmation that could be mistaken for a transaction.
- **“What is the actual point?” Medium risk in the current close, which repeats privacy and fictional-download details before landing the joke.** The audience needs one crisp thesis, not a second feature list: premium packaging can monetize a capability the phone already has.

## Recommended three-minute arc

| Time | Speaker | Job and cue |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Keep the restrained mirror timeline and layered phone reveal. No narration; title at :25. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Serious product reveal, then the turn: “The glass is conveniently the screen you already own.” Avoid unsupported “ultra-clear” claims. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Show Silver partial-screen, then Gold full-screen. Let the visual prove the joke. Add one privacy sentence; give the permission prompt / toggle a pause. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Silver free; Gold ₹2,000; ₹5,000 extras with one or two best gags. Point to the clearly fictional 25 GB / 10 GB copy. State no transactions or downloads occur; make free exit legible. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | One sentence on the thesis, briefly confirm local/temporary camera behavior and free exit, then close: “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That’s the whole point.” |

## Short answer for judges

**“We built a working front-camera mirror demo: Silver shows a partial-screen reflection, Gold expands it to full-screen, and the camera preview is optional, local, and temporary. The upgrades, fees, and huge-download claims are fictional. It’s useless because it sells back a basic feature the phone already has.”**

## Rehearsal fixes

The script is plausible at a brisk pace, but the live toggles and handoffs need room. Rehearse to a hard 3:00 with the actual deck, one person advancing slides, and a no-camera run. If late, trim the extras list and redundant privacy/download lines—not the demo, no-purchase/free-exit clarification, or final thesis. Make speaker handoffs happen on slide transitions. The “one trial” is specified in the build prompt but not the spoken pitch; omit it unless the UI visibly demonstrates it, since explaining it adds little to the core joke.

## Assumptions

I treated the stated three-minute flow and four-speaker assignment as fixed, and reviewed the current script, slide notes, storyboard, and implementation cues. I assume the actual app will be run locally and that all satirical pricing/download UI remains nonfunctional.
