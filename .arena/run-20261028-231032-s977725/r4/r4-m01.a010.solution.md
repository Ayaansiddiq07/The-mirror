# The Mirror — judge-proof pitch review

## Likely judge objections and fixes

- **“Is this a built app or only a camera preview with pricing jokes?” — Real risk.** Establish the working artifact and visibly switch Silver (partial-screen) to Gold (full-screen). That concrete change is the proof of what was built.
- **“What if camera access is denied?” — Real demo risk.** Keep the supplied local fallback ready. Say, “Camera access is unavailable, so we’re using the local fallback illustration,” then continue the explanation. Do not claim the illustration responds to the Silver/Gold controls unless rehearsal confirms that behavior.
- **“What happens to the camera feed?” — Real trust risk.** Say once: “The preview is temporary and stays on this device. We do not record, upload, or retain camera data.” Confirm this matches the implementation before presenting.
- **“Are the prices and download claims real?” — Real clarity risk.** Label the pricing and claims as fictional, and say no purchase or download occurs. Gold is a mock one-time ₹2,000 upgrade; extras are mock UI at ₹5,000. The 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months are fictional. Call the ₹2,000 deletion fee a dismissible joke and keep closing free.
- **“Does the 30-second film earn its place?” — Pacing risk.** It uses one sixth of the pitch before the product is named, but that slot is specified. Make the premium mood immediately legible, keep it silent, and land the title near 00:25 so the spoken reveal starts at 00:30.
- **“Can four speakers finish in three minutes?” — Manageable rehearsal risk.** Use the handoffs at :30, 1:00, 1:45, and 2:25. Rehearse the actual controls and pauses. If long, trim the extras list and repeated explanations first; preserve the build statement, privacy assurance, fictional-price clarification, and closing point.

## Focused pitch structure

| Time | Speaker | Beat |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1 (silent) | Reflection in water → mirror history → phone reveal; title near :25. No narration or audio. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Serious premium reveal, ending with the screen-you-already-own joke. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | State what was built; switch Silver to Gold; give the privacy assurance. If denied, use the local fallback illustration without promising an unverified toggle. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Silver free; mock one-time ₹2,000 Gold; mock ₹5,000 extras; fictional download/update claims; dismissible fake deletion fee and free exit. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | Briefly restate the local temporary preview, the useless premise, and the point. |

## Suggested spoken script

**00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal:** “Introducing The Mirror: an elegant front-camera reflection experience, powered by the camera already on your phone. We engineered every detail, including the glass—which is conveniently the screen you already own.”

**01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo:** “We built a local front-camera mirror with two display modes. Silver gives you part of the screen; Gold gives you all of it.” *(Switch modes.)* “Same camera, same reflection, same phone—now the empty space has ambition. The preview is temporary and stays on this device. We do not record, upload, or retain camera data.” **If camera access is denied:** “Camera access is unavailable, so we’re using the local fallback illustration.” Continue the explanation without claiming that the illustration changes modes.

**01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing:** “Silver is free. Gold is a fictional one-time ₹2,000 to unlock the rest of your own screen. The ₹5,000 extras are fictional too. So are our 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months: nothing is purchased or downloaded. The fake ₹2,000 deletion fee is dismissible. Closing stays free.”

**02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close:** “The demo uses a temporary local camera preview, with no recording, upload, or retained personal data. We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the whole point of useless software. Thank you.”

**Short answer — “What did you build and why is it useless?”** “We built a temporary, local front-camera mirror with partial-screen Silver and full-screen Gold modes. It is useless because Gold jokes about charging you to use the rest of your screen to show the same reflection.”

## Assumptions

The supplied timings are fixed; the assumed one trial is a one-time first-run preview; and the team will verify that the privacy statement matches the implementation. The fallback line describes only the confirmed local illustration. No unstated judging rubric is assumed.
