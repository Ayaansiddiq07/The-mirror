## Review

The core gag is clear and pitchable: the phone already has a mirror, while The Mirror sells access to the screen it already uses. The strongest objections are practical: the 30-second film takes a sixth of the pitch before the app appears; the script risks explaining every gag after the audience has understood it; and the privacy and fictional-pricing details need to be unmistakable in a live demo. Keep the serious premium reveal and monochrome tone, but make the app visible by 00:30, make the demo itself prove the joke, and end on the short “phone that already had one” line.

## Likely judge questions and risk

- **“Is this a usable app or only a deck?” — Highest risk.** The pitch promises a live demo. Actually toggle Silver and Gold, and show the same preview expanding from partial to full screen. Have the local illustration ready if permission is denied or the camera is unavailable; say plainly that the fallback is what the audience is seeing. Avoid calling it “instant” or “ultra-clear,” which the app cannot guarantee across devices.
- **“Is the camera safe, and what if I say no?” — High trust risk, easy to address.** The current script says local and not saved, but make the complete promise once: “With permission, the preview stays on this device temporarily; we do not record, upload, or retain it. If you decline, the illustration stays on screen.” Don’t imply the live camera works when showing the fallback.
- **“Are these charges and downloads real?” — High clarity risk if the screen is ambiguous.** The ₹2,000 Gold price and ₹5,000 extras are funny only if obviously mock and no payment can happen. Keep the spoken “No purchase happens,” and label the controls “mock / no payment.” Mark 25 GB and 10 GB updates “fictional—no download”; label the deletion fee a joke and keep “Close for free” prominent. Mention these absurd claims once, not in both pricing and closing.
- **“Why spend 30 seconds on a film for a mirror?” — Moderate pacing risk.** It supports the serious-to-satirical reveal, so retain the silent film, but make it a brisk visual setup with no historical detours: reflection → phone → title. Reveal THE MIRROR near 00:25 and have Speaker 1 start immediately at 00:30.
- **“Can four speakers land this in three minutes?” — Moderate rehearsal risk.** The listed slots add to exactly 3:00, leaving no transition or demo recovery time. The spoken copy is plausible at a clear pace, but the demo and pauses consume time. Rehearse against a hard clock; target about 10 seconds of slack by trimming feature lists and the closing recap. Keep the privacy assurance, no-payment clarification, and final point.

## Recommended arc and wording

| Time | Speaker | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Premium visual setup; title at about 00:25. No narration or loud audio. |
| 00:30–00:55 | 1 | Serious product reveal, then pivot: “The glass is conveniently the screen you already own.” |
| 00:55–01:40 | 2 | Show Silver partial screen, then Gold full screen. Deliver the local-camera and denied-permission explanation in one concise line. |
| 01:40–02:15 | 3 | Silver is free; Gold is a mock one-time ₹2,000; extras are mock ₹5,000. Land “unlimited angles—which is still one angle.” State no purchase happens. |
| 02:15–02:50 | 4 | One quick fictional-download/deletion-fee beat with free exit visible; state the point and thank the audience. |
| 02:50–03:00 | — | Hold for the closing line and applause; avoid adding more explanation. |

Suggested final answer to **“What did you build and why is it useless?”**: “We built a phone mirror using the front camera, with a partial-screen Silver mode and full-screen Gold mode. It’s useless because the phone already has a front camera—and we pretend the empty screen space is a premium upgrade.”

The current close repeats privacy, both fictional data-size claims, deletion pricing, free exit, the thesis, and thanks in 35 seconds. Keep the safe-use facts available on screen, say the fictional download and deletion gags briefly if they are part of the demo, then close with: **“We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the whole point of useless software. Thank you.”**

**Assumptions:** The slot timings and four-speaker arrangement are fixed; the film is part of the required reveal. The UI labels and fallback described in the pitch/site are implemented as stated. This review treats the prices, download claims, and deletion fee as fictional satire, consistent with the assignment.
