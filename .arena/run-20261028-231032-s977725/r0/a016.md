# The Mirror: judge-proof pitch review

## Open questions and working assumptions

- **Does the 30-second opening earn its time?** It takes one sixth of the pitch, but the brief requires a serious premium reveal before the joke. Keep it if the film is a tight, silent 30 seconds with a clear title reveal at 00:25; avoid adding narration or extra setup.
- **Is the product genuinely demoable?** The current implementation supports Silver/Gold UI changes and an optional `getUserMedia` preview, with an illustration fallback. Describe this accurately: the tier change is a visual UI change, not a technical camera upgrade.
- **Can the privacy claim be supported?** The reviewed app code stops camera tracks when leaving the demo, hiding the page, closing the dialog via free exit, or unloading. Say the *camera preview* stays local and temporary; do not imply the whole presentation is offline (the opening can load an external image and the page references hosted fonts).
- **What does “one trial” mean?** Assume it is a one-time first-run preview, not a recurring trial or a paywall on the basic mirror. Explain only if asked; do not add a new mechanic to the pitch.

## Recommended three-minute arc

| Time | Speaker | Purpose and delivery |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Retain water → mirrors → phone → product name at 00:25. Silent, restrained, no flashing or loud audio. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Serious premium reveal; land “glass is the screen you already own.” About 45–55 words. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Toggle Silver then Gold; let each visual change register. Explain the joke through the unchanged camera and person. Give the privacy/fallback line while the demo remains visible. About 65–75 words including cues. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Read the free, ₹2,000, and ₹5,000 tiers as escalating absurdity. Show the 25 GB and 10 GB claims as clearly fictional UI; say no purchase or download happens. About 70–80 words. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | State what the team built, why it is useless, and close on the existing mirror punchline. No new feature list. About 55–65 words. |

These word ranges leave room for the mode toggles and speaker handoffs. The current draft’s last section is especially crowded: it repeats camera privacy and fictional download claims already established, then explains the premise and closes. Trim that repetition first. Keep a visible timer or timed cue cards, and have Speaker 2 advance directly into the first price card at 01:45.

## Suggested tightened spoken copy

**00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1:** “Introducing The Mirror: a purpose-built reflection experience, powered by the front camera already on your phone. Instant. Ultra-clear. Carefully engineered—including the glass, which is conveniently the screen you already own.”

**01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2:** “Silver gives your reflection a tasteful partial-screen window, leaving the rest for nothing. Now Gold: same front camera, same person, same phone—just a full-screen reflection. We charged the empty space with ambition. Camera permission is optional. The preview stays on this device; we don’t record or upload it, and leaving the demo stops the camera. If permission is denied, this illustration is our fallback.”

**01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3:** “Silver is free: half the screen, all the reflection. Gold is a one-time ₹2,000 to unlock the rest of your own screen. For ₹5,000, get a confidence filter, unlimited angles—which is still one angle—and a cloth for the glass that was already there. The 25-gigabyte download and 10-gigabyte updates are fictional. So are the prices: these are mock buttons, with no payment or download.”

**02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4:** “We built a working mirror app with Silver and Gold display modes, an optional local camera preview, and a fallback when the camera is unavailable. We wrapped an ordinary phone feature in absurd premium packaging to show how easily software can sell back what you already have. The deletion fee is a joke; closing is always free. We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is useless software. Thank you.”

The copy is intentionally compact; rehearse at presentation pace with the camera permission prompt and toggles. If it runs long, cut the “instant / ultra-clear / carefully engineered” trio and the extras list before cutting the privacy line, fictional-price clarification, free-exit assurance, or final punchline.

## Small presentation fixes

- Change the extras button from **ADD PREMIUM EXTRAS** to **EXTRAS — MOCK ONLY**. Its current success toast (“Premium extras activated”) can sound like a real entitlement; change it to “Satire only. No purchase made.”
- Keep **MOCK ONLY** visible on the Gold button and include it on the extras button. The spoken clarification should cover both prices and fictional download claims.
- Keep the deletion fee clearly secondary to the free exit. Dismissal and closure must remain available; never make the gag resemble a real device lock or purchase flow.
- Don’t claim “4K” or “ultra-clear” as a measured technical capability. If retained in the straight-faced reveal, present it as obvious launch-copy satire, not a promise.

## Short answer: what did you build, and why is it useless?

“We built a phone mirror app with partial- and full-screen modes and an optional temporary local camera preview. It is useless because the phone already has a front camera—and we pretend that ordinary reflection needs premium tiers, fees, and updates.”

The monochrome premium tone and serious-to-satirical reveal are strong. The safest version preserves that reveal, demonstrates real interface behavior, and makes the fictional commerce and limited camera handling unmistakable in one pass.
