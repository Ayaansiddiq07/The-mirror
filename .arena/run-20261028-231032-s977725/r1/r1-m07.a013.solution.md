# The Mirror: judge-proof pitch review

## Strongest likely judge objections

1. **Will the live demo fit the slot? Manageable, but rehearse it.** The 30-second film leaves 150 seconds for four spoken sections, interface actions, pauses, and handoffs. The script's roughly 280 spoken words are not by themselves evidence of a high overrun risk; at a clear pace the schedule is plausible. The uncertainty is how long the Silver-to-Gold switch, camera permission, and transitions take. Rehearse with the actual interface and a hard 3:00 timer. Keep a short buffer by cutting feature-list jokes first.

2. **Is there a real app behind the mock pricing? Medium-high risk.** Show the actual Silver-to-Gold screen change and, when permission is granted, a visibly live camera preview. Say that the ₹2,000 and ₹5,000 controls are mock UI and cannot charge anyone. The Extras button currently reads “ADD PREMIUM EXTRAS” while Gold says “MOCK ONLY”; label both equally clearly as mock controls.

3. **Does the privacy explanation match the implementation? High trust risk, easy to address.** Say: “The preview stays on this device; we do not record, upload, or retain it.” Be prepared to show that the stream stops when the demo ends or the app closes. Do not imply the camera is on before permission. Keep the camera-denied fallback visible and usable.

4. **Could the satire look like a real paywall or hostile exit? Medium risk.** Label the 25 GB download, 10 GB updates, upgrade prices, and ₹2,000 deletion fee as fictional where they appear. The deletion gag needs an obvious dismiss action and free close action. Do not make the audience hunt for the free exit. If the trial is mentioned, clarify that it is a one-time first-run preview.

5. **Does the 30-second opening earn its share of the pitch? Medium risk.** It uses one sixth of the slot. Keep the image progression legible and reveal the title at 00:25. It should establish a serious premium mood, then let the ordinary phone camera and paid empty space expose the joke. Keep it silent as planned.

## Recommended pitch structure and handoffs

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film.** Water → glass → phone; title at 00:25. No setup narration.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal.** Sell the polished premise and end with “the glass is conveniently the screen you already own.”
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo.** Show Silver, switch to Gold, and pause long enough for the same reflection to fill the screen. Give the local-camera/privacy line once. If permission is denied, use the fallback and continue.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing.** Silver free; Gold ₹2,000 once; extras ₹5,000. Land one or two jokes, then say: “All prices and download claims are fictional; no purchase or download occurs.”
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close.** Mention the dismissible deletion-fee gag only if it is shown. Avoid repeating the download sizes and full privacy statement. End by 02:55 to leave room for thanks.

Queue the next slide before each handoff. If rehearsal runs long, drop “confidence filter,” the imaginary forecast, and repeated technical detail before trimming demo visibility or the final line.

## Short answer for judges

“We built a phone app that uses the front camera as a temporary mirror, then satirically charges for making that same reflection full-screen. It is useless because the phone already has a camera and a screen; the premium tiers sell back empty space.”

## Suggested closing

“We built a mirror for a phone that already had one. The camera preview stays local and temporary; every price and download claim was a joke, and closing is free. That is the whole point of useless software. Thank you.”

**Assumptions:** The four speakers each take one main segment, with Speaker 1 also introducing the film. The live demo uses the existing local preview and illustration fallback. The one trial means a first-run preview shown once, not a repeating trial or purchase prompt.
