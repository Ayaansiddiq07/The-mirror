# The Mirror: pitch review

## Assumptions and open questions

I reviewed the supplied script and the matching presentation notes in `index.html`. I assume the described Silver/Gold controls and camera-denied fallback work in the running demo; the team should only claim functionality it can demonstrate. The brief fixes the five pitch slots, four speakers, and the meaning of “one trial” as a one-time first-run preview. No judging rubric is supplied, so these are plausible judge questions, not claimed criteria.

The key questions are whether the joke lands without over-explaining, the app is genuinely demoable, the privacy and mock pricing are clear, the opening earns 30 seconds, all four handoffs fit, and the ending is crisp. A further pricing clarity check: if “trial” appears in the interface or spoken pitch, describe it as a one-time first-run preview; avoid suggesting a recurring or paid trial.

## What is working

The premise is easy to grasp: a phone app sells access to the mirror the phone already has. The Silver-to-Gold switch demonstrates the gag instead of merely describing it, and the straight-faced launch tone gives the satire room to land. The script also includes important safeguards: optional camera permission, no recording or upload, fictional download and upgrade claims, a dismissible deletion-fee gag, and a free exit.

## Likely objections and risk

- **“Is this a real app or just a pitch deck?” High risk.** The script calls it a live demo, but the close could say more plainly what the team actually built. Show Silver and Gold changing the preview and name the implementation accurately: “We built a working mirror app with Silver and Gold display modes” if that is true. If trial language appears, clarify that it is a one-time first-run preview.
- **“What happens to the camera feed?” High risk for trust.** “Temporary and local” is useful but abstract. At the permission prompt say: “The preview stays on this device while the screen is open; we don’t record, upload, or retain it.” Ensure the preview stops when the demo closes. Keep the line factual and short.
- **“Could those prices or the deletion fee actually charge me or block me?” High risk if the UI is ambiguous.** Keep “mock only” / “no purchase happens” next to the upgrade controls, including the ₹5,000 extras. If showing the deletion-fee dialog, make “joke” and “CLOSE FOR FREE” immediately visible; dismissal and exit must remain easy. If a trial is shown, label it a one-time first-run preview so it cannot read as recurring billing.
- **“Why spend 30 seconds on the intro?” Medium risk.** The water-to-phone film establishes the serious reveal, but a history montage could feel like filler before a simple app. Keep it silent, visually changing, and purposeful; land THE MIRROR at 00:25 and transition promptly. The film earns its time only if polished and rehearsed.
- **“Will four speakers fit?” Medium risk.** A mode toggle, pause, and handoffs consume the 2:30 speaking time. The close repeats details already covered. Rehearse to the clock and trim repeats before cutting the Silver/Gold proof or final line.

**Suggested tighter close:** “We built a working mirror app with Silver and Gold modes to show how a basic feature gets dressed up as premium software. The camera preview is temporary and local; nothing is recorded, uploaded, or retained. The fees and download claims are jokes, and closing is always free. We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That’s the point. Thank you.”

**What did you build, and why is it useless?** “We built a working phone mirror with free partial-screen Silver and a mock ₹2,000 full-screen Gold upgrade. It is useless because the phone already has a front camera, and Gold sells you the rest of your screen.”

## Synthesis

The strongest case for the current pitch is that the serious launch voice makes the escalating prices funny, while the live mode switch and safeguards make the idea tangible. The strongest objection is that the 30-second film and repeated explanation can crowd out the actual proof and flatten the ending. Keep the cinematic opening and premium tone, but center the live switch, state privacy and mock-payment facts once where they matter, and use the close to name the build and its point. Any “trial” mention should mean only the one-time first-run preview. This keeps the joke intact while answering practical questions.
