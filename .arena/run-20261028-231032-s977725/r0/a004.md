# The Mirror: judge-proof pitch review

## Read on the concept

The core joke is clear and demoable: Silver and Gold change how much of the screen shows the same ordinary front-camera reflection, while the product language and prices inflate that trivial change. The premium monochrome reveal supports the satire. The strongest ending is already in the script: “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one.” Keep that as the final idea, and let the audience laugh before “Thank you.”

## Likely judge objections and fixes

- **“Is this a real app or just a mockup?” — Genuinely risky.** The live camera and tier controls are the proof, so make both changes visibly on cue and have the local illustration ready if permission is denied. Say once, plainly: “The preview is temporary and stays on this device; we don’t record, upload, or retain it.” Don’t imply camera access is required.
- **“Are those charges or downloads real?” — Genuinely risky if unclear.** The ₹2,000 Gold upgrade, ₹5,000 extras, 25 GB download, and 10 GB updates every two months are fictional. Keep “MOCK ONLY” and “FICTIONAL — NO DOWNLOADS OR PURCHASES” legible while those claims are shown. Say “No purchase happens” next to the pricing gag. The ₹2,000 deletion fee must remain dismissible, with “CLOSE FOR FREE” visible; never delay or block exit.
- **“Does the joke need explaining?” — Some risk.** The script explains the premise in the reveal, pricing, and close. Trust details are useful, but repeating them at the end softens the punchline. Deliver the privacy assurance during the demo, clarify fictional pricing during the pricing slide, and reserve the close for the point of the app.
- **“Does the 30-second silent film earn its time?” — A real pacing risk, not a concept flaw.** It takes one-sixth of the pitch before judges see the app. Keep the serious-to-satirical reveal, but make the visual progression purposeful and land on the actual phone and THE MIRROR title by 00:25. If rehearsal shows the sequence feels like generic product footage, shorten the montage within the opening slot and give those seconds to the live demo.
- **“Can four speakers fit?” — Rehearsal risk.** The written sections are plausible at a brisk pace, but slide cues, laughter, toggle delays, and handoffs consume time. Rehearse against a hard 03:00 timer, with the demo fallback and transitions included. Trim feature lists first; do not rush the privacy, mock-only, or final-point lines.

## Recommended three-minute arc

| Time | Speaker | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Restrained mirror-to-phone film; title lands at 00:25. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Serious reveal; one crisp screen-already-is-the-glass joke. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Toggle Silver to Gold; show same reflection, then give the one-sentence privacy assurance. Use the local illustration if camera access is denied. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Deliver the escalating fictional prices and extras; keep the download/update claims visibly labeled fictional and say no transaction occurs. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | One sentence on what the app demonstrates, optionally point to the dismissible fee/free exit, then end on the existing mirror-for-a-phone line. |

**Short answer to “what did you build and why is it useless?”** “We built a phone app that turns its front camera into a mirror, then sells screen space and imaginary extras as premium upgrades. It’s useless because the phone already has a screen and a camera—and the same reflection.”

## Assumptions

I treated the prices, download size, update cadence, and deletion charge as fictional UI jokes only; no payment, download, data retention, or exit restriction is intended. The 30-second silent opening and four-speaker sequence are fixed constraints.
