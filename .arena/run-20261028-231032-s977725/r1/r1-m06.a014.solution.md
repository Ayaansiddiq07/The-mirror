# The Mirror — judge-proof pitch review

The satirical engine is clear: the app does something a phone already does, then sells back screen space and imaginary extras. Keep the polished, serious reveal, make the Gold toggle the first unmistakable joke, and keep every fictional charge visibly harmless.

## Likely judge objections

- **“Is there a real app here, or just a launch video?” — genuinely risky.** Use the live mode controls in the demo: Silver shows a partial-screen reflection and Gold expands it to full screen. The camera preview is a one-time first-run trial. If that preview has already been used, or permission is denied or unavailable, show the local illustration fallback and demonstrate the same mode change. Describe only what the build visibly does.
- **“Are these fees or downloads real?” — genuinely risky if the UI is ambiguous.** Before interacting, say the tiers are mock pricing; keep “NO PURCHASES OR DOWNLOADS” visible. The ₹2,000 Gold upgrade, ₹5,000 extras, 25 GB download claim, and 10 GB updates every two months are fictional UI and must trigger no purchase or download. The ₹2,000 deletion fee is a dismissible joke; show that closing is always free.
- **“What happens to the camera feed?” — manageable, but precision matters.** State once that the optional preview stays on-device and temporary, with no recording, upload, or retained personal data. Keep the camera-denied fallback ready. The one-time first-run trial means a repeat presentation should use that fallback rather than imply the camera trial can be restarted.
- **“Does the joke arrive too late?” — a real pacing risk.** The silent film uses 30 of 180 seconds. Keep it only if it earns the premium-product reveal and reaches the title on cue. By the end of the first spoken section, reveal that the “glass” is the existing screen; let the Silver-to-Gold toggle land the laugh. If rehearsal runs long, shorten the historical montage or feature list, not the demo or free-exit clarification.
- **“Can four speakers fit?” — manageable with rehearsal.** Keep one speaker per spoken section and assign slide advancement to a speaker who can do it without extra narration. Rehearse with the actual transitions; leave a beat for the toggle. The 30-second close should not repeat the entire privacy and fictional-download explanation.

## Focused three-minute arc

| Time | Beat and job |
|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | Silent monochrome reflection-to-phone film. Reveal THE MIRROR by 00:25; hold the title for the handoff. |
| 00:30–01:00 | Serious premium reveal, then expose that the “glass” is the screen already on the phone. |
| 01:00–01:45 | Show Silver partial screen → Gold full screen. Use the one-time first-run camera preview only if available; otherwise show the local illustration. Say the preview is optional, temporary, and local, with no recording, upload, or retention. |
| 01:45–02:25 | Label the tiers and extras as mock pricing before interaction. Show the fictional download/update claims and no-purchase/no-download label. |
| 02:25–03:00 | Dismiss the deletion-fee joke, show free close, then deliver the thesis. |

**Suggested close (about 50 words):** “Silver is free; Gold is a mock ₹2,000 to unlock the rest of your own screen. The extras, download, updates, and deletion fee are jokes; there are no purchases, downloads, or paid exits. We built a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the point.”

**Answer to “what did you build and why is it useless?”** “We built a phone app that can show a temporary front-camera mirror, then satirizes premium software by charging—in mock UI—for the rest of the screen and imaginary upgrades. It is useless because the phone already has a camera and a screen.”

## Assumptions

The listed timings and required product behaviors are fixed. The camera preview is a one-time first-run trial; subsequent demos use the local illustration fallback. I assume the Silver/Gold controls, mock pricing, free exit, and camera-denied fallback behave as described. Do not claim a demo behavior unless the team has verified it in the build. No unstated judging rubric is assumed.
