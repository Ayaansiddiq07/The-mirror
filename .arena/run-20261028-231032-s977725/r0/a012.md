# The Mirror: pitch review

## What judges are most likely to question

1. **Can you actually demo it?** The concept is intentionally simple, so a permission prompt, blank preview, or missed toggle could become the story. Rehearse the camera prompt and both modes; open the app ready to go. Ask for camera access only when Speaker 2 starts, and have the illustration visible and ready if access is denied or unavailable. Keep the explanation precise: the preview is local and temporary, with no recording, upload, or retention. Say this only if the implementation does exactly that.
2. **Is this a joke or a real paywall?** The premise is clear by the “screen you already own” line, but the pricing interface could briefly look real. Label all prices, buttons, the 25 GB download, and 10 GB updates as fictional/mock in the UI and in Speaker 3’s first pricing line. Never trigger a purchase or download. Explain that “one trial” means one first-run preview, not a recurring trial or a real lockout.
3. **Does the deletion fee trap the audience?** The script says it is dismissible and exit is free, which is good. Make “joke” and “CLOSE FOR FREE” readable immediately; don’t make the fee a required demo step. The free-exit line should be the last pricing gag the audience hears.
4. **Can the close fit?** Speaker 4 has 82 words in 35 seconds, before pauses or the fee interaction. That is a rushed finish. The planned 30-second film also takes one-sixth of the pitch; if its mirror-history montage delays the phone reveal, it spends time without proving the app exists.

## Recommended adjustments

Keep the monochrome, premium reveal. End the film with the actual app/phone and title by 00:25, leaving five seconds for the reveal. At 00:30, make the first sentence sound premium; land the joke by the end of Speaker 1’s section. In the demo, show Silver then Gold, and explicitly point out that only the screen area changes. Use the fallback rather than troubleshooting live.

Trim Speaker 4 to the point and the trust facts: “We built a temporary, local front-camera preview. It records and uploads nothing. The giant download, updates, and deletion fee are all jokes; you can close for free. We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the point of useless software.” This is 43 words, leaving room for pauses and a visible free-exit action. In Speaker 3’s opening, say “These are mock prices; there is no checkout.” Then give the ₹2,000 Gold upgrade and ₹5,000 extras, choosing one or two strongest extras rather than rushing the whole list. If the deletion gag is shown, dismiss it and visibly choose free exit.

## Focused three-minute arc

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film:** Water to glass to phone; actual app and title appear by 00:25.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal:** Sell the polished reflection experience seriously; end on “the glass is the screen you already own.”
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo:** Optional camera/fallback, Silver partial screen, Gold full screen, same reflection. One sentence on local temporary preview.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing:** State mock/no-checkout first; land ₹2,000 Gold and ₹5,000 extras, then one download/update absurdity. Keep fee gag visibly dismissible and exit free.
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close:** One concise privacy statement, fictional claims/free exit, then the core point. Rehearse with actual mode changes and pauses against the clock.

**Short answer:** We built a phone app that shows a temporary front-camera mirror, with a partial-screen Silver mode and full-screen Gold mode. It is useless because the phone already has a front camera—and the premium upgrades merely charge for using more of the screen.
