# The Mirror: pitch review

## Likely judge objections and practical fixes

1. **Is the app genuinely built, and will the demo work? Medium-high risk.** The premise depends on seeing the same reflection change from Silver’s partial-screen view to Gold’s full-screen view. Rehearse that exact transition in the running app, including camera permission and the denied-permission fallback. Keep the fallback ready so a permission prompt or unavailable camera does not consume the 45-second demo slot. Let the visual change carry the joke; do not add extra feature explanations during the toggle.

2. **Does the camera explanation earn trust? High trust risk, easy to resolve.** State once, during the demo: “With permission, the preview stays on this device temporarily; we do not record, upload, or retain it.” Do not suggest the camera is active before permission is granted. Show the fallback when permission is denied, and be ready to show that the preview stops when the demo ends. This is a meaningful implementation detail, not just part of the satire.

3. **Could the mock prices or exit gag be mistaken for real charges? Medium risk.** Keep “mock” or “fictional” visible beside both the ₹2,000 Gold upgrade and ₹5,000 extras. The deletion-fee gag must be dismissible, and the app’s close action must remain obviously free. Label the claimed 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months as fictional wherever they appear; they must not initiate downloads. If the one trial is mentioned, call it a one-time first-run preview.

4. **Does the 30-second film justify one sixth of the pitch? Medium risk.** Preserve the serious premium reveal, but make the visual progression readable without narration and reveal THE MIRROR at 00:25. The turn into the joke should happen immediately in the product reveal: the “glass” is the phone screen the audience already owns. The film is silent as specified; remove any room-tone or soundtrack ambiguity.

5. **Can four speakers fit the three-minute limit? Manageable, but rehearse the handoffs.** The film consumes 30 seconds, leaving 150 seconds for the four spoken sections and the live interaction. The planned sections total exactly 150 seconds, so there is no spare slot for a slow permission prompt, extended pauses, or repeated explanations. Run the whole pitch with the actual interface and a hard 3:00 timer. If it runs long, cut the confidence-filter and forecast jokes first; keep the demo, fictional-price clarification, privacy line, and final point.

## Focused pitch structure

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film:** Water → glass → phone; layered product reveal; title at 00:25.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal:** Deliver the polished product claim, then puncture it with “the glass is conveniently the screen you already own.”
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo:** Show Silver, switch to Gold, and let the same reflection fill the screen. Give the one-sentence privacy explanation. Use the camera-denied fallback without stopping.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing:** Silver is free; Gold is a mock one-time ₹2,000; extras are mock UI at ₹5,000. Land one or two jokes, then say clearly that all prices and download claims are fictional and trigger no purchase or download.
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close:** State what the team built and why it is useless. Mention the dismissible deletion-fee gag only if it is shown. Finish with the free-exit clarification and a crisp final line; avoid repeating the full privacy and download explanations.

Queue the demo screen before the Speaker 1-to-2 handoff and pricing screen before the Speaker 2-to-3 handoff. Keep the demo interaction visible long enough for judges to understand the Silver/Gold difference.

## Short answer for judges

“We built a phone app that uses the front camera as a temporary mirror, then satirically charges to make the same reflection full-screen. It is useless because the phone already has a camera and a screen; the premium tiers sell back empty space.”

## Suggested closing

“We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. Every upgrade and download claim was fictional, and closing is free. That is the whole point of useless software. Thank you.”

**Assumptions:** Each of the four speakers owns one spoken section after Speaker 1’s silent film. The demo uses the app’s local camera preview and its camera-denied fallback. The “one trial” means a one-time first-run preview, not a purchase prompt or recurring trial.
