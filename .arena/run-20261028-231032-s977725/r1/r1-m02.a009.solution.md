# The Mirror — judge-proof pitch review

## Open questions to settle before presenting

1. **Does the running app match the privacy claims?** The supplied script and presentation describe a temporary local preview and a camera-denied fallback, but this review has not audited runtime code. Verify that there is no recording, upload, retention, or personal-data logging. If the team cannot verify that, do not make an absolute privacy claim on stage; describe only what has been checked and use the fallback.
2. **Does the 30-second film land on time and earn its slot?** The script calls for the title at 00:25, but the film itself was not reviewed here. Rehearse it against a timer. If the reveal arrives late or the history montage feels generic, trim the montage while preserving the serious premium setup and satirical reveal.
3. **Can live controls and speaker handoffs fit?** The outline allocates all three minutes with no spare time. Rehearse the real toggle and handoffs. If camera permission or a control stalls, show the camera-denied illustration and continue.
4. **Can viewers distinguish the satire from real transactions?** Ensure mock prices and download sizes are visibly labeled as fictional, with no purchase/download action. This is a presentation/UI check, not something established by the spoken script alone.

These are unresolved checks, not assumed failures. The recommendations below assume the app behaves as specified in the assignment and that any stage claim is verified against the running build.

## Strongest likely judge objections, ranked by risk

1. **“Is this a built app or only a polished presentation?” — High risk.** The demo promises actual Silver/Gold controls. Start by using them, immediately and visibly: Silver shows a partial-screen preview; Gold expands that same preview to full screen. Call these the working behaviors. Treat the ₹5,000 extras and pricing screens as mock UI, not implemented services. If the camera does not start promptly, use the fallback rather than spending the demo waiting for permission.
2. **“What happens to the camera feed?” — High trust risk if unclear; low if verified and said once.** One plain line during the demo is enough: “Camera access is optional. The preview stays on this device for this session; we do not record, upload, or retain it.” Use this only if it is true of the actual build, including logs. Keep the denied/unavailable fallback visible and usable.
3. **“Are these prices, download sizes, or fees real?” — Medium risk.** ₹2,000 for Gold, ₹5,000 for extras, 25 GB to download, 10 GB updates every two months, and the ₹2,000 deletion fee are jokes. Put “FICTIONAL · NO PURCHASE OR DOWNLOAD” by the mock prices and download claims; make controls produce only a joke confirmation. Say “These prices and sizes are mock UI; nothing is purchased or downloaded.” Keep the deletion fee dismissible and “CLOSE FOR FREE, ALWAYS” visible. Closing must work without paying or being trapped.
4. **“Does the 30-second opening justify its share of the pitch?” — Medium, dependent on execution.** It consumes one-sixth of the slot before speech. Keep the elegant silent build, but make it brisk and land THE MIRROR by 00:25 so the immediate spoken reveal can puncture the premium tone. Cut any visual history beat that does not build toward the phone. Do not borrow from the demo if the film overruns; shorten the film.
5. **“Will four speakers fit the clock?” — Medium.** The allocated blocks use the entire three minutes. The script has short spoken sections, but toggles, laughter, and handoffs take time. Rehearse with the actual interface and a timer. Trim the extra-feature list and repeated disclaimers first; preserve one verified privacy sentence, one clear fictional/no-transaction sentence, and the final point. Assign hard handoff cues.

## Suggested spoken wording

**Product reveal (00:30–01:00):** “Introducing The Mirror: an elegant reflection experience, powered by the front camera already on your phone. We engineered the glass too. Conveniently, it is the screen you already own.”

**Demo (01:00–01:45):** “Silver gives your reflection a tasteful partial-screen window. Gold expands it to the full screen. Same camera, same reflection; we have simply put a price on the empty space. Camera access is optional. The preview stays on this device for this session; we do not record, upload, or retain it.” Use the privacy sentence only after verifying the implementation. If camera permission is denied, show the fallback and move on.

**Pricing (01:45–02:25):** “Silver is free. Gold is a one-time ₹2,000 to unlock the rest of your own screen. For ₹5,000, the extras bundle includes a confidence filter, unlimited angles—which is still one angle—and a cloth for the glass that was already there. The prices and download sizes are fictional mock UI; nothing is purchased or downloaded.” Keep the 25 GB and 10 GB-every-two-months claims on screen only if there is time to label them clearly.

**Close (02:25–03:00):** “There is even a fake ₹2,000 deletion fee. Dismiss it and close for free, always. We built a phone mirror that charges you to reveal screen space your phone already had. That is why it is useless. Thank you.”

The one-trial preview is a single first-run preview, not a recurring trial. Do not make the deletion gag obstruct exit. Keep the app usable without locking the device, collecting personal data, flashing rapidly, or playing loud audio.

## Direct answer: what did you build, and why is it useless?

“We built a phone mirror using the front camera, with a partial-screen Silver mode and a full-screen Gold mode. The camera preview is optional, temporary, and local, with a fallback if access is denied. It is useless because Gold charges ₹2,000 to reveal screen space your phone already had.”

Use the privacy clause only if verified in the running app; otherwise say only the verified behavior.
