# The Mirror — judge-proof pitch review

## Strongest likely judge objections

1. **Is the app genuinely built and demoable? — High risk.** The pitch promises a live Silver/Gold toggle. Make that the first visible action in the demo: Silver shows a partial-screen reflection; Gold expands the same preview to full screen. If camera permission is denied or the preview is unavailable, show the local illustration and continue without waiting. Be precise about which features work: the modes are demo behavior; the premium extras and fees are jokes in mock UI.

2. **What happens to the camera feed? — High trust risk if the answer is vague or unverified.** The script says optional, temporary, local, and not recorded, uploaded, or retained. Confirm those statements against the running build before saying them, including that no personal data is retained in app logs. Then state the verified behavior once, plainly, during the demo. A fallback should remain usable when permission is denied.

3. **Are the prices, download sizes, and deletion fee real? — Medium risk.** Label the ₹2,000 Gold upgrade, ₹5,000 extras, 25 GB download, 10 GB updates every two months, and ₹2,000 deletion fee as fictional mock UI. No control should purchase or download anything. Keep the deletion gag dismissible and make “Close for free, always” legible; the exit must never depend on payment.

4. **Does the 30-second film earn its share of the pitch? — Medium, execution-dependent.** It uses one-sixth of the total slot before anyone speaks. Keep the serious monochrome build and the water-to-phone history, but make every beat advance toward the reveal; land THE MIRROR at 00:25 as planned. If rehearsal shows the title or reveal arriving late, trim the montage rather than the live demo.

5. **Can four speakers fit three minutes? — Medium.** The schedule has no buffer, and the demo includes two mode changes. Rehearse with the actual interface and a timer, including handoffs and likely laughter. Trim the extra-feature list and forecast line first. Preserve the serious-to-satirical reveal, one verified privacy sentence, an unmistakable no-transaction clarification, and the ending.

## Assumptions and limits

This review treats the assignment’s product behavior as the intended design, but does not independently verify the app, film, or stage timing. Privacy wording is appropriate only after checking the actual build. The 30-second film’s effectiveness depends on its execution. The objections above are practical questions implied by the brief, not claims about an unstated judging rubric.

## Focused pitch structure

| Time | Speaker | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Premium monochrome film; title by 00:25; no loud audio or rapid flashing. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Serious product reveal, ending on the “glass” being the screen already owned. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Demonstrate Silver then Gold. Give one verified privacy statement. Use the denied-camera fallback promptly if needed. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Present the mock ₹2,000 and ₹5,000 prices and one or two best extras jokes. Clearly mark prices and fictional download claims as satire; say no purchase or download occurs. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | Dismissible deletion-fee joke, free exit, and a crisp statement of what was built and why it is useless. |

## Suggested spoken wording

**Product reveal (00:30–01:00):** “Introducing The Mirror: an elegant reflection experience, powered by the front camera already on your phone. We engineered the glass too. Conveniently, it is the screen you already own.”

**Demo (01:00–01:45):** “Silver gives your reflection a tasteful partial-screen window. Gold expands it to the full screen. Same camera, same reflection; we have simply put a price on the empty space. Camera access is optional. The preview stays on this device for this session; we do not record, upload, or retain it.” Use the final two sentences only if verified in the actual build. If permission is denied, show the fallback and continue.

**Pricing (01:45–02:25):** “Silver is free. Gold is a one-time ₹2,000 to unlock the rest of your own screen. For ₹5,000, the extras bundle includes a confidence filter, unlimited angles—which is still one angle—and a cloth for the glass that was already there. These prices and download sizes are fictional; nothing is purchased or downloaded.” The 25 GB and 10 GB-every-two-months claims may remain in mock UI if clearly labeled and the team has time to make them readable.

**Close (02:25–03:00):** “There is even a fake ₹2,000 deletion fee. Dismiss it and close for free, always. We built a phone mirror that charges you to reveal screen space your phone already had. That is why it is useless. Thank you.”

The first-run preview is a one-time trial, not a recurring trial. Keep camera access optional and the preview local and temporary. Provide a usable denied-permission fallback. Do not record, upload, or retain personal data. The app must not lock the device; the deletion fee must never block exit; fictional size claims must not trigger downloads; and the experience must avoid rapid flashing and loud audio.

## Direct answer: what did you build, and why is it useless?

“We built a phone mirror using the front camera, with a partial-screen Silver mode and a full-screen Gold mode. The preview is optional and temporary, with a fallback if camera access is denied. It is useless because Gold charges ₹2,000 to reveal screen space your phone already had.”

Include the claim that preview data stays local and is not recorded, uploaded, or retained only after verifying it in the running app.

## Requirements checklist

1. **Identify likely judge objections, explain their risk, and recommend fixes:** addressed in the five ranked objections above, including demo reality, privacy, satire clarity, opening-film value, and timing.
2. **Keep the satirical premise and serious-to-satirical reveal:** retained in the film, reveal wording, mode demo, mock pricing, and final line.
3. **Give focused timing and speaker structure:** provided in the 00:00–03:00 table for all four speakers, including the silent 30-second opening.
4. **Answer what was built and why it is useless:** provided directly in the short answer and closing wording.
5. **State assumptions:** the review explicitly says it has not independently verified the implementation, film, or timing, and conditions privacy claims on checking the build.
6. **Honor specified behavior and safety constraints:** calls for temporary local camera preview, denied-permission fallback, no personal-data retention, fictional non-transactional pricing and size claims, a dismissible deletion gag, always-free exit, one-time first-run trial, and no device locking, rapid flashing, or loud audio.
7. **Do not invent judging criteria:** objections are presented as likely practical questions, not an assumed rubric.
