# The Mirror — judge-proof pitch review

## Likely judge objections and fixes

- **“Is this a built app or just a camera preview with pricing jokes?” — Real risk.** Establish the working artifact before the gag: “We built a local front-camera mirror with two display modes.” Then visibly switch Silver (partial-screen) to Gold (full-screen). That change is the demo’s proof.
- **“What happens if the camera is denied?” — Real demo risk.** Keep the local illustration ready and say, “Camera access is optional; this illustration shows the same Silver-to-Gold change.” Rehearse the fallback so a permission prompt cannot derail the demo.
- **“What happens to the camera feed?” — Real trust risk.** Say once during the demo: “The preview is temporary and stays on this device. We do not record, upload, or retain camera data.” Confirm this wording against the implementation.
- **“Are these prices and download claims real?” — Real clarity risk.** Make “fictional mock UI” visible and say that no purchase or download occurs. Name the fake ₹2,000 deletion fee aloud, say it is dismissible, and keep “Close for free” visible. Gold’s ₹2,000 is a mock one-time upgrade; premium extras are mock UI at ₹5,000. The claimed 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months must not trigger downloads.
- **“Is the 30-second film worth the time?” — Pacing risk.** It is one sixth of the pitch before the product is named, but the supplied format fixes that slot. Make the serious premium mood legible quickly, keep the film silent through 00:30, and reveal the title at the end so speech can begin on time.
- **“Can four speakers fit three minutes?” — Manageable with timed rehearsal.** Use the handoffs at :30, 1:00, 1:45, and 2:25. Rehearse with the actual UI switch and pauses. If over time, trim the extras list and repeated explanation first; retain the working-product statement, privacy assurance, explicit fictional-pricing clarification, and final point.

## Suggested spoken script

**00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal:** “Introducing The Mirror: an elegant front-camera reflection experience, powered by the camera already on your phone. We engineered every detail, including the glass—which is conveniently the screen you already own.”

**01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo:** “We built a local front-camera mirror with two display modes. Silver gives you part of the screen; Gold gives you all of it.” *(Switch modes.)* “Same camera, same reflection, same phone—now the empty space has ambition. The preview is temporary and stays on this device. We do not record, upload, or retain camera data.” If camera access is denied: “Camera access is optional; this illustration shows the same Silver-to-Gold change.”

**01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing:** “Silver is free. Gold is a fictional one-time ₹2,000 to unlock the rest of your own screen. The ₹5,000 extras are fictional too. So are our 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months: nothing is purchased or downloaded. The fake ₹2,000 deletion fee is dismissible. Closing stays free.”

**02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close:** “The demo uses a temporary local camera preview, with no recording, upload, or retained personal data. We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the whole point of useless software. Thank you.”

**Short answer — “What did you build and why is it useless?”** “We built a temporary, local front-camera mirror with partial-screen Silver and full-screen Gold modes. It is useless because Gold jokes about charging you to use the rest of your screen to show the same reflection.”

## Assumptions

The supplied timings are fixed; the assumed one trial is a one-time first-run preview; and the team can verify that the privacy statement matches the implementation. The review uses the supplied assignment and does not assume any additional competition criteria.
