# The Mirror: judge-proof pitch review

## Strongest likely objections

1. **Does the joke land clearly, and does the app actually work? — High risk.** The premise is intentionally simple, so an unclear reveal or failed camera permission prompt can consume the pitch. The serious opening should turn into an unmistakable joke by the first spoken section, and the demo should visibly show Silver as partial-screen and Gold as full-screen. Rehearse those controls and keep the camera-denied illustration ready. Do not claim implementation details beyond what the team can demonstrate.

2. **Are the camera and privacy claims trustworthy? — High risk if vague or overlong.** State once, plainly, that the preview is temporary and local, with no recording, upload, or retention. Make permission optional and show the fallback if denied. The claim builds trust; repeating it in both demo and close crowds out the joke, so reserve the full explanation for the demo and a short reminder only if needed in the close.

3. **Could fictional prices, downloads, or deletion fees be mistaken for real actions? — High risk.** Label prices and the 25 GB download / 10 GB updates as fictional mock UI, and say there is no checkout or actual download. Keep the ₹2,000 deletion gag dismissible; visibly choose “Close for free.” Never make it a required action. “One trial” means a one-time first-run preview, not a paid trial.

4. **Does the 30-second film earn one sixth of the slot? — Meaningful risk, not automatically a flaw.** It can establish the premium tone that makes the reveal work, but a long mirror-history montage delays proof of the app. Keep the assigned silent slot only if it shows the actual app by 00:20, holds the product/title through 00:30, and uses each image to build the reveal. If it remains only atmosphere, shorten the montage within the available slot and let the app appear sooner. Do not add narration or loud audio.

5. **Can four speakers and the transitions fit? — Manageable with hard cues.** Three handoffs at three seconds each cost nine seconds. The block plan below reserves that time, as well as mode-toggle pauses; keep scripts within the listed word caps and cut optional feature jokes before cutting the privacy, no-purchase, or free-exit clarifications.

## Recommended three-minute arc and compact script

The timings below include transitions and actions. Word counts are ceilings; speak clearly and leave the listed visual beats unhurried.

| Time | Speaker and budget | Action / purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | Speaker 1 silent | Premium, silent film. Move quickly from reflection imagery to the real app by 00:20; reveal THE MIRROR and hold through 00:30. |
| 00:30–01:00 | Speaker 1, up to 48 words | Serious product reveal, then the screen-you-already-own joke. |
| 01:00–01:45 | Speaker 2, up to 66 words | Allow 3 seconds for handoff, about 8 seconds for Silver/Gold toggles, and the rest for explanation. |
| 01:45–02:25 | Speaker 3, up to 58 words | Allow 3 seconds for handoff. Show fictional prices and one absurd claim, then free exit if the deletion gag is shown. |
| 02:25–03:00 | Speaker 4, up to 48 words | Allow 3 seconds for handoff; close on the uselessness point and stop at 03:00. |

**00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1:** “Introducing The Mirror: a premium reflection experience, powered by the front camera already on your phone. We refined every detail, including the glass—which is conveniently the screen you already own.”

**01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2:** “Silver gives your reflection part of the screen. Now Gold fills the whole display. Same camera, same reflection; we simply charged the empty space with ambition. The preview is temporary and local, with no recording, upload, or retention. If camera access is denied, we can show the fallback.”

**On screen:** Toggle Silver, then Gold. Use the fallback illustration if camera access is denied or unavailable. Keep the preview local and temporary.

**01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3:** “Silver is free. Gold is a mock one-time ₹2,000 upgrade to unlock your own screen. The mock ₹5,000 extras include unlimited angles—which is still one angle. These prices are jokes: there is no checkout. Our fictional 25 GB download and 10 GB updates never download anything.”

**On screen:** Mark prices and download/update claims “fictional.” If the dismissible ₹2,000 deletion-fee gag appears, dismiss it and visibly select “Close for free.” Do not linger on the interface.

**02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4:** “We built a temporary, local front-camera mirror. It is useless because your phone already has a camera and screen; Gold sells you the rest of your screen. The download and deletion fee are jokes. Closing is free. That is the point of useless software. Thank you.”

## What we built and why it is useless

“We built a phone app that shows a temporary, local front-camera mirror, with a partial-screen Silver mode and full-screen Gold mode. It is useless because the phone already had the camera and screen, and Gold charges to use more of the screen.”

## Assumptions

The five listed time blocks and silent 30-second opening are the intended format. “One trial” is a one-time first-run preview. The brief specifies local camera behavior and fallback; this review has not independently verified the implementation. The script’s word caps assume about 100–115 spoken words per minute after transition and demo time is reserved; rehearse with a stopwatch and trim optional jokes if needed.
