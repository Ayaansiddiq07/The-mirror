# The Mirror — pitch review

The concept lands when the demo makes the upgrade visibly absurd: Silver shows a partial-screen reflection; Gold expands the same reflection to the full screen. Keep the polished monochrome reveal serious until that switch, then let the pricing expose the joke.

## Likely judge objections and fixes

- **“Is this just a camera preview with pricing jokes?” — Real risk.** Name the working product at the start of the demo: “We built a local front-camera mirror with two display modes.” Then show Silver and Gold. The behavior change is the proof that there is a built app behind the satire.
- **“Does it work if camera access is denied?” — Real live-demo risk.** Ask permission only when demonstrating the camera. If access is denied or unavailable, leave the local illustration visible and say: “Camera is optional; this illustration shows the same Silver-to-Gold change.” Rehearse this path so a permission prompt does not consume the demo.
- **“What happens to the camera feed?” — Real trust risk.** State once, during the demo: “The preview is temporary and stays on this device; we do not record, upload, or retain camera data.” This is clearer than saying only that nothing is saved. Keep the wording aligned with the implementation.
- **“Are the prices and download claims real?” — Real clarity risk.** Label the ₹2,000 Gold upgrade, ₹5,000 extras, 25 GB download, and 10 GB updates every two months as fictional mock UI. Say upgrade buttons do not purchase anything and the file-size claims trigger no downloads. Make the ₹2,000 deletion fee dismissible and keep “Close for free” visible. Describe the assumed trial as a one-time first-run preview if it comes up; avoid implying recurring billing.
- **“Does the opening earn 30 seconds?” — A pacing risk.** The film is fixed and silent, and it uses one sixth of the pitch before the product is named. Keep the reveal legible and end on the title exactly at :30. Don’t add narration or audio. Start the first spoken line on the title cue and preserve the actual demo time.
- **“Can four speakers fit the slot?” — Manageable through rehearsal.** The current ending repeats privacy, download, and deletion details already covered. Cut those repeats from the close. Use clear handoff cues at :30, 1:00, 1:45, and 2:25; rehearse with the demo transitions included. If over time, trim the extras list before cutting the product explanation or final line.

## Focused three-minute structure

| Time | Speaker and beat | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | Speaker 1: silent film | Water reflection → mirror history → layered phone reveal → title at :25. No narration and no audio for all 30 seconds. |
| 00:30–01:00 | Speaker 1: product reveal | Deliver the premium description straight-faced. End on the idea that the “glass” is the phone screen already owned. |
| 01:00–01:45 | Speaker 2: demo | Identify the local front-camera mirror, show Silver then Gold, deliver the short privacy line. Use the local illustration if camera access fails. |
| 01:45–02:25 | Speaker 3: satirical pricing | Silver free; Gold mock one-time ₹2,000; extras mock ₹5,000. Label all prices and file-size claims fictional; state no purchase or download occurs. Show the dismissible deletion gag and free exit. |
| 02:25–03:00 | Speaker 4: close | State the point in one sentence, then thank the audience. Add no new feature or safety explanation. |

**Short answer to “what did you build and why is it useless?”** “We built a temporary, local front-camera mirror with a partial-screen Silver mode and a full-screen Gold mode. It is useless because Gold jokingly charges you to use the rest of a screen to show the same reflection.”

## Suggested wording and close

At the demo opening: “We built a local front-camera mirror with two display modes. Silver gives you part of the screen; Gold gives you all of it.”

Privacy line: “The preview is temporary and stays on this device; we do not record, upload, or retain camera data.”

Pricing setup: “These are fictional prices in mock UI. Nothing is purchased or downloaded.” Keep the deletion fee dismissible and the free-close control clear on screen.

Close: “We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is the whole point of useless software. Thank you.”

Assumptions: The supplied timing is fixed, the first-run preview happens once, and the review is based on the supplied script and product details rather than an unstated competition rubric. The team should verify that the spoken privacy claims match the actual implementation.
