# The Mirror — pitch review

## Assessment

The core joke is strong and legible: the phone already has a front camera, while The Mirror sells screen area and imaginary premium value back to its owner. Silver/Gold makes that joke visible in a live demo, and the final line is a good button. Keep the monochrome, serious-launch delivery; let the absurdity come from the claims and prices.

## Judge objections to prepare for

- **“What did you actually build?” — genuinely risky.** The script spends more time describing the proposition than establishing what runs. During the demo, show the real front-camera preview if permission is granted, visibly switch from partial to full screen, and say that these are the app’s two working modes. If permission is denied, name the illustration as a fallback rather than presenting it as live camera footage. Don’t claim a feature that the demo cannot show.
- **“Is the camera recording or sending anything?” — trust-sensitive and genuinely risky.** “Nothing is recorded or saved” is reassuring but vague. Prefer the precise line: “The preview stays on this device while the demo is open; we don’t record, upload, or retain it.” Keep camera permission optional and demonstrate the fallback. Avoid implying that a browser permission prompt itself proves privacy.
- **“Are those prices, download sizes, or the deletion fee real?” — risky if labels are missed.** The spoken line that buttons are jokes helps, but the 25 GB/10 GB claim is currently only an on-screen disclaimer. Say “fictional” as it appears. Label the ₹5,000 bundle button mock-only too, and make the deletion-fee screen plainly dismissible with a visible free exit. Keep the free-exit joke; it sharpens the premise.
- **“Why spend 30 seconds on a film?” — a real trade-off, not a fatal flaw.** It is one-sixth of the pitch and delays proof that the app exists. Keep it if the cinematic serious-to-satirical reveal is central, but reveal THE MIRROR by 00:25 and use the final five seconds to land on a recognizable phone/app image. Don’t add narration or extend the slot.
- **“Can four speakers finish on time?” — genuinely risky at the close.** The current closing is about 78 words in 35 seconds before pauses, transitions, and the deletion-fee gag; that is tight. The demo and pricing sections also need room for controls and audience laughter. Rehearse to the fixed clock, not just the written word count.

The page’s “AI-ENHANCED YOU” and “4K” reveal graphics could read as actual capabilities. Keep them visibly tongue-in-cheek or label them as mock claims; don’t let judges spend the Q&A asking where the AI is.

| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Silver free, Gold ₹2,000, extras ₹5,000; deliver one or two best gags. Explicitly say prices and buttons are mock UI, and fictional sizes cause no downloads. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | One brief thesis, deletion-fee/free-exit gag, crisp final line. Aim for 50–55 spoken words and leave a beat for the laugh. |

## Suggested spoken close (52 words)

“We built a working front-camera mirror with two modes: Silver shows part of the screen; Gold fills it. The preview stays on-device and is never recorded or uploaded. The prices, download sizes, and deletion fee are jokes; leaving is free. We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. Thank you.”

Use the existing serious reveal and pricing patter, but cut feature-list words before cutting the demo or the explicit mock-only/privacy clarifications. Keep the transition cues visible to speakers so the four handoffs do not consume their allotted lines.
