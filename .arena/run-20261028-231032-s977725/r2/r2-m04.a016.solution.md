# The Mirror: judge-proof pitch review

## Open questions and working assumptions

- **Does the 30-second opening earn its time?** It takes one sixth of the pitch, but the brief calls for a serious premium reveal before the joke and specifies a silent 30-second film. Keep the visual arc, with no soundtrack, narration, or sound effects, and reveal THE MIRROR by 00:25.
- **Is the product genuinely demoable?** The presentation supports Silver/Gold UI changes and an optional `getUserMedia` preview, with an illustration fallback. Describe this accurately: Gold changes the area of the preview, not the camera itself.
- **Can the privacy claim be supported?** The camera preview stays local and temporary. Say only that the preview is not recorded or uploaded and that the camera stops when leaving the demo; do not imply the entire presentation is offline or make an unsupported claim about all website data.
- **What does “one trial” mean?** Assume it is a one-time first-run preview, not a recurring trial or paywall on the basic mirror. It need not be a pitch beat unless a judge asks.

## Recommended three-minute arc

| Time | Speaker | Purpose and delivery |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Water → mirrors → phone → THE MIRROR at 00:25. Complete silence throughout; no narration, soundtrack, or sound effects. Use steady visuals with no rapid flashing. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Serious premium reveal; land “glass is the screen you already own.” About 45–55 words. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Toggle Silver then Gold; explain the unchanged camera/person and growing window. Give the concise privacy and fallback line while the demo remains visible. About 65–75 words including cues. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Present free, ₹2,000, and ₹5,000 tiers as escalating absurdity. Show the fictional 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months; say no payment or download occurs. About 70–85 words. |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | State what the team built, why it is useless, and close on the mirror punchline. No new feature list. About 50–65 words. |

The ranges leave room for toggles and handoffs. The close should not repeat camera privacy and fictional download claims already covered. Keep the update cadence in pricing; use a visible timer or timed cue cards, and have Speaker 2 advance directly to the first price card at 01:45.

## Suggested tightened spoken copy

**00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1:** “Introducing The Mirror: a purpose-built reflection experience, powered by the front camera already on your phone. Instant. Ultra-clear. Carefully engineered—including the glass, which is conveniently the screen you already own.”

**01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2:** “Silver gives your reflection a tasteful partial-screen window, leaving the rest for nothing. Now Gold: same front camera, same person, same phone—just a full-screen reflection. We charged the empty space with ambition. Camera permission is optional. The preview stays on this device; we don’t record or upload it, and leaving the demo stops the camera. If permission is denied, this illustration is our fallback.”

**01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3:** “Silver is free: half the screen, all the reflection. Gold is a one-time ₹2,000 to unlock the rest of your own screen. For ₹5,000, get a confidence filter, unlimited angles—which is still one angle—and a cloth for the glass that was already there. The 25 GB download and 10 GB updates every two months are fictional. So are the prices: these are mock buttons, with no payment or download.”

**02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4:** “We built a working mirror app with Silver and Gold display modes, an optional local camera preview, and a fallback when the camera is unavailable. We wrapped an ordinary phone feature in absurd premium packaging to show how easily software can sell back what you already have. The deletion fee is a joke; closing is always free. We made a mirror for a phone that already had one. That is useless software. Thank you.”

This copy is compact; rehearse at presentation pace with the camera permission prompt and toggles. If it runs long, cut the “instant / ultra-clear / carefully engineered” trio and the extras list before cutting the privacy line, fictional-price clarification, update cadence, free-exit assurance, or final punchline.

## Small presentation fixes

- Change the extras button from **ADD PREMIUM EXTRAS** to **EXTRAS — MOCK ONLY**. Its current success toast (“Premium extras activated”) can sound like a real entitlement; change it to “Satire only. No purchase made.”
- Keep **MOCK ONLY** visible on the Gold and extras buttons. The spoken clarification should cover both prices and fictional download claims.
- Keep the deletion fee clearly secondary to the free exit. Dismissal and closure must remain available; never make the gag resemble a device lock or purchase flow.
- Don’t claim “4K” or “ultra-clear” as a measured technical capability. If retained in the straight-faced reveal, make it read as launch-copy satire, not a promise.
- If the mock download card appears, show the full claim accurately: **25 GB download; 10 GB updates every two months**. Neither claim triggers a download.

## Short answer: what did you build, and why is it useless?

“We built a phone mirror app with partial- and full-screen modes and an optional temporary local camera preview. It is useless because the phone already has a front camera—and we pretend that ordinary reflection needs premium tiers, fees, and updates.”

Preserve the monochrome premium tone and serious-to-satirical reveal. Demonstrate real interface behavior, make fictional commerce and the limited camera handling unmistakable, and keep the opening completely silent.
