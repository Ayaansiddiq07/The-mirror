# The Mirror pitch review

## Strongest likely judge objections

- **“Is there a real, demoable product here?” — a genuine risk if the team only describes the joke.** Show Silver and Gold changing the preview on the actual app: Silver uses part of the display; Gold expands the same preview to full-screen. Keep the fallback ready so the demo works if camera access is denied. Describe only behavior that is actually implemented.
- **“What happens to camera data?” — a genuine trust risk because the product asks for camera access.** Say plainly: “Camera access is optional. The preview stays on this device temporarily; we do not record, upload, or retain it.” The on-screen fallback should be ready if permission is denied or the camera is unavailable.
- **“Are the prices and download claims real?” — a genuine clarity risk if mock UI resembles a transaction.** Put “MOCK ONLY · NO PURCHASE” on the ₹2,000 Gold and ₹5,000 extras controls. Label the 25 GB download, 10 GB updates every two months, and ₹2,000 deletion fee as fictional mock UI. The deletion-fee joke must be dismissible, and “CLOSE FOR FREE” must be clear. If the mock interface includes a trial, call it “ONE-TIME FIRST-RUN PREVIEW”; otherwise omit trial wording.
- **“Does the opening film earn 30 seconds?” — a real pacing tradeoff.** Preserve the serious premium reveal, but land the title by 00:25 and make the phone unmistakable before the spoken reveal. In rehearsal, shorten the montage before cutting the demo or privacy line.
- **“Will the close fit?” — a real rehearsal risk.** The spoken script currently adds privacy, fake download sizes, the deletion-fee gag, and the thesis after pricing. Keep the download and deletion-fee details as on-screen jokes during the pricing beat; the final 35 seconds can then carry the point and one concise privacy reminder only if needed.

The serious-to-satirical turn is strong: “the glass…is conveniently the screen you already own” reveals the premise, and “charged the empty space with ambition” gives the demo its best punchline. Keep the monochrome premium tone. Make labels such as “AI-ENHANCED YOU” and “YOUR FACE, IN 4K” visibly absurd or clearly part of fictional mock product language so they are not mistaken for real camera claims.

## Focused three-minute arc

| Time | Speaker | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–00:30 | 1, silent | Serious reflection-to-phone film; title by 00:25. |
| 00:30–01:00 | 1 | Reveal that the “advanced glass” is the screen the audience already owns. Let the turn land once. |
| 01:00–01:45 | 2 | Demonstrate Silver then Gold; deliver “same reflection, more ambition” and the short privacy sentence. Use the illustration if camera access is unavailable. |
| 01:45–02:25 | 3 | Keep the existing spoken pricing copy to Silver free, Gold ₹2,000 once, and extras ₹5,000. Say explicitly that the buttons are jokes and no purchase happens. Show the fictional download, update, and deletion-fee gags as labeled mock UI without adding spoken lines. Mark any trial “ONE-TIME FIRST-RUN PREVIEW.” |
| 02:25–03:00 | 4 | State what was built and why it is useless; end on the thesis. Do not introduce more feature or pricing details. |

Use one speaker per spoken segment, with Speaker 1 carrying the reveal. Speaker 2 operates the mode controls while speaking. Rehearse to the listed cutoffs, especially the 45-second demo and final 35 seconds; preserve short pauses for the interface changes.

## Short answer: what did you build, and why is it useless?

“We built a phone app that turns its front camera into a mirror, then sells the same reflection back in premium tiers. Silver uses part of the screen; Gold uses all of it. The preview is temporary and local. The upgrades and fees are jokes, and closing is always free. It is useless because the phone already had a mirror.”

## Assumptions

The listed segment timings are fixed; the camera behavior in the brief is the intended implementation. The team can display the fictional download, update, and deletion-fee gags in mock UI without narrating them, and can label or omit trial language. No unstated judging rubric is assumed.
