# The Mirror: judge-proof pitch review

## The main risks

1. **The three-minute slot may run over. High risk.** The 30-second film leaves 150 seconds for speech, demo actions, pauses, and four handoffs. The script is roughly 280 spoken words before the demo pauses; that is feasible at a brisk pace, but the closing repeats privacy and fictional-download explanations after the pricing section already established them. Rehearse against a hard 3:00 timer with the actual Silver/Gold toggle and transitions. Keep 5–8 seconds of recovery time; cut the feature list before cutting the point.

2. **A judge may wonder whether there is a real app behind the mock pricing. Medium-high risk.** Show the actual Silver-to-Gold screen change, and make the camera preview visibly live when permission is granted. If permission is denied, use the existing illustration fallback without apologizing. Say explicitly that the ₹2,000 and ₹5,000 controls are mock UI and cannot charge anyone. The Extras button currently reads “ADD PREMIUM EXTRAS” while Gold says “MOCK ONLY”; make both equally explicit on screen.

3. **Privacy claims invite technical follow-up. High trust risk, easy to address.** “Nothing is recorded or saved” is reassuring only if the implementation matches it. Say “the preview stays on this device; we do not record, upload, or retain it,” and be ready to show that the stream stops when the demo ends or the app closes. Do not imply the camera is on before permission. Keep the camera-denied fallback visible and usable.

4. **The satire could be mistaken for a real paywall or hostile exit. Medium risk.** Label the 25 GB download, 10 GB updates, upgrade prices, and ₹2,000 deletion fee as fictional at the moment they appear. The deletion gag must have an obvious dismiss and a free close action. Never make the audience hunt for the free exit. Do not use “trial” as a recurring acquisition loop: if mentioned, clarify it is a one-time first-run preview.

5. **The opening film takes a meaningful share of the pitch. Medium risk.** Thirty silent seconds is one sixth of the slot. It earns its place if the image progression is legible and the title reveal lands at 00:25; it becomes dead time if the sequence feels like generic product mood footage. Preserve the serious premium reveal, then let the ordinary phone camera and paid empty space expose the joke. Keep it silent as planned.

## Recommended timing and handoffs

- **00:00–00:30 — Speaker 1, silent film.** Water → glass → phone; title at 00:25. No extra setup.
- **00:30–01:00 — Speaker 1, reveal.** Sell the polished premise, ending on “the glass is conveniently the screen you already own.”
- **01:00–01:45 — Speaker 2, demo.** Show Silver, switch to Gold, pause long enough to see the same reflection fill the screen. Deliver the local-camera/privacy line once. Use the fallback immediately if permission is denied.
- **01:45–02:25 — Speaker 3, pricing.** Silver free; Gold ₹2,000 once; extras ₹5,000. Land one or two jokes, then state “All prices and download claims are fictional; no purchase or download occurs.”
- **02:25–03:00 — Speaker 4, close.** Mention the dismissible deletion-fee gag only if it is shown. Do not re-explain the download sizes or repeat the full privacy statement. End by 02:55 to leave room for thanks.

Rehearse each speaker’s handoff with the next slide already cued. If timing slips, drop “confidence filter,” the imaginary forecast, and repeated technical detail before trimming demo visibility or the final line.

## Short answer for judges

**“We built a phone app that uses the front camera as a temporary mirror, then satirically charges for making that same reflection full-screen. It is useless because the phone already has a camera and a screen; the premium tiers sell back empty space.”**

## Suggested closing

“We built a mirror for a phone that already had one. The camera preview stays local and temporary; every price and download claim was a joke, and closing is free. That is the whole point of useless software. Thank you.”

**Assumptions:** The four speakers each take one main segment, with Speaker 1 also introducing the film. The live demo uses the existing local preview and illustration fallback. The one trial means a first-run preview shown once, not a repeating trial or a purchase prompt.
